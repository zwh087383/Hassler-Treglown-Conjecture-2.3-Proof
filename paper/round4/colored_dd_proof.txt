# Colored dominating dissociation sets: independent audit and full-link application

Date: 2026-11-22. Authoring/auditing agent: round4_global.

STATUS. The counting theorem was proposed by the root agent and independently
checked by both round4_global and round4_zero. The proof below is self-contained.
The minimum-degree argument is simplified using round4_zero's observation that
the red-degree tie-break handles both the degree-two and degree-three cases.
No claim of Lean verification of this new theorem is made.

THE MAIN CONSEQUENCE is a uniform, exact link-graph bound for every sum-free seed
S=T union {s} with T=-T. It does not require bounded seed size or bounded rank,
and does not leave an exceptional-core error. It does not cover arbitrary seeds
with two or more unmatched antipodal elements.

1. Statement
------------

Let Q and R be simple graphs on the same finite vertex set V, and let G=Q union R.
Let C(Q,R) be the number of subsets D of V satisfying all three conditions:

  (i) D is independent in Q;
  (ii) the induced red graph R[D] has maximum degree at most one;
  (iii) D dominates G: every vertex outside D has a neighbour in D in G.

THEOREM. If R is triangle-free, then

  C(Q,R) <= 3^(|V|/2).                            (1.1)

Equality holds precisely when G is a disjoint union of red edges and Q is empty,
including the empty graph on zero vertices.

An edge belonging to both Q and R can be removed from R without changing the
count. Indeed condition (i) forbids both endpoints from lying in D, so that edge
never occurs in R[D]; it remains present in G through Q. Removing it preserves
triangle-freeness. Hence it suffices to prove the theorem for disjoint edge sets
E(Q) and E(R).

For an induced vertex set W, write C(G[W]) for the corresponding count using the
inherited blue and red edge colours. All induced subgraphs again have triangle-
free red graph. The empty graph on zero vertices has count one. The count is
multiplicative over connected components of G.

2. The general branching inequality
-----------------------------------

For every vertex v,

  C(G) <= C(G-v) + C(G-N_G[v])
          + sum_{w in N_R(v)} C(G-(N_G[v] union N_G[w])).        (2.1)

Here and below N_G[v] is the closed neighbourhood, and deletion is induced.

Proof. A counted D not containing v remains a counted set in G-v: it dominates
every remaining vertex, and the two induced-subgraph conditions persist. This
gives an injection into the first term; sets counted there need not dominate v,
so only an inequality is asserted.

If v belongs to D, condition (i) and condition (ii) imply that v has either zero
or exactly one neighbour in G[D]. With zero neighbours, all other vertices of D
lie outside N_G[v], and D\{v} is counted in G-N_G[v]. Conversely every counted
set of that smaller graph extends by v to this case.

With one neighbour, it must be a red neighbour w. The pair {v,w} is an isolated
edge in G[D], because blue adjacency is forbidden and red degree is at most one.
Every remaining selected vertex lies outside N_G[v] union N_G[w], and the rest
of D is counted in that induced subgraph. Again the converse extension is valid.
Different red partners give disjoint cases. This proves (2.1).

3. Proof when the minimum degree is at least two
------------------------------------------------

Set alpha=sqrt(3), and induct on N=|V|. Let delta be the minimum degree in G.
Among vertices of degree delta, choose v with its red degree r=d_R(v) as small
as possible. Assume delta>=2.

CASE A: r<=delta-1.

All closed neighbourhoods N_G[v] union N_G[w] in (2.1) contain N_G[v], which has
delta+1 vertices. By the induction hypothesis,

  C(G)/alpha^N <= alpha^(-1)+delta*alpha^(-delta-1).              (3.1)

For integers delta>=2, delta*alpha^(-delta-1) decreases: the ratio of successive
terms is (delta+1)/(delta*alpha)<=3/(2sqrt(3))<1. Thus (3.1) is at most

  alpha^(-1)+2alpha^(-3)=5sqrt(3)/9 < 1,

where the final strict inequality follows from 75<81.

CASE B: r=delta.

All edges incident with v are red. For every red neighbour w, we claim

  |N_G[v] union N_G[w]| >= delta+2.              (3.2)

If d_G(w)>=delta+1, this is immediate from |N_G[w]|>=delta+2. If instead
d_G(w)=delta, the choice of v implies d_R(w)>=r=delta, hence every edge incident
with w is also red. As vw is red and R is triangle-free, v and w have no common
neighbour in G. Therefore

  |N_G[v] union N_G[w]|=2delta>=delta+2.

This proves (3.2). Applying induction in (2.1) now gives

  C(G)/alpha^N <= alpha^(-1)+alpha^(-delta-1)
                                      +delta*alpha^(-delta-2).  (3.3)

The last two terms equal (alpha+delta)*alpha^(-delta-2), which decrease with
delta>=2. Indeed the successive ratio is

  (alpha+delta+1)/(alpha*(alpha+delta)) < 1,

because (alpha-1)(alpha+delta)>1. Hence (3.3) is at most its value at delta=2,

  alpha^(-1)+alpha^(-3)+2alpha^(-4)
    = (2+4sqrt(3))/9 < 1.

The last inequality is equivalent to sqrt(3)<7/4, or 48<49. This completes all
minimum-degree-at-least-two cases, without a separate degree-three exception.

4. Isolated vertices and the exact leaf recurrence
--------------------------------------------------

If G has an isolated vertex v, every dominating set contains v, and
C(G)=C(G-v)<=alpha^(N-1)<alpha^N.

Now let v be a leaf with unique neighbour w, and let d_G(w)=D. Put
c=2 if vw is red and c=1 if vw is blue. Then the following identity holds:

  C(G) = C(G-{v,w}) + c*C(G-N_G[w])
          + sum_{u in N_R(w)\{v}}
                      C(G-(N_G[w] union N_G[u])).              (4.1)

To verify it, first consider v selected and w unselected. Removing v and w
leaves exactly a counted set of G-{v,w}, because v has no other neighbours.
Next consider w selected. If it has no selected red partner, the count is
C(G-N_G[w]). If it has a red partner u different from v, it gives the displayed
sum. Finally v and w may both be selected exactly when vw is red; that produces
a second copy of C(G-N_G[w]). Domination of the leaf excludes the case in which
neither v nor w is selected. These are disjoint, exhaustive cases.

If D=1, the pair {v,w} is an isolated component K2. It contributes three choices
when red ({v},{w},{v,w}), and two choices when blue ({v},{w}). Thus its count is
at most alpha^2, with equality exactly for a red edge.

If D>=2, every deleted closed-neighbourhood union in (4.1) contains N_G[w], of
size D+1. There are at most D-1 possible red partners other than v. Therefore

  C(G)/alpha^N <= alpha^(-2)+(D+1)alpha^(-D-1).

The second term decreases for integers D>=2, so the right side is at most

  1/3+3alpha^(-3)=(1+sqrt(3))/3 < 1.

The blue-leaf case has an even smaller coefficient. This proves (1.1) in all
cases. Every step is strict unless it removes an isolated red K2 component.
Multiplicativity therefore also proves the equality characterization. QED.

5. The full-link application: notation and paired loops
------------------------------------------------------

Let H be a finite F5-vector space, G0=F5 x H, and B={2,3} x H. Let S be a
sum-free subset of G0\B of the form

  S=T union {s},   T=-T.

If s belongs to T, the seed is already symmetric. Otherwise -s is not in S;
we treat this single unmatched antipodal element. Zero does not belong to S,
because a sum-free set has no solution x+y=z even with repeated entries.

The link graph on B has an ordinary edge between distinct x,y if one of x+y,
x-y,y-x belongs to S. A vertex x has a loop exactly when

  x in (S+S) union (S-S) union (S/2).

The looped vertices form a negation-invariant set. Here is an exact verification,
which removes the need to discard only a bounded exceptional core. Because T=-T,

  S+S = (T+T) union (T+s) union {2s},
  S-S = (T+T) union (T+s) union (T-s) union {0}.

Also T/2 is contained in T+T: in characteristic five, t/2=3t=(-t)+(-t).
Finally s/2=3s=-2s. Since zero is outside B, the looped set is precisely

  B intersect [(T+T) union (T+s) union (T-s) union {2s,-2s}].     (5.1)

Every set in the bracket is paired under negation, taking the middle two sets
together. Thus deleting all looped vertices deletes entire orbits {x,-x}.

Write W for the remaining vertices. No orbit {x,-x} has an internal edge in the
remaining simple link: its sum is zero, while a difference equal to an element
of S would give 2x in S or -2x in S, forcing at least one of x,-x to be looped;
by (5.1), both would already have been deleted.

6. Quotient by antipodal pairs
------------------------------

Parametrize the antipodal pairs by x in H:

  A_x=(2,x),   B_x=(3,-x),   orbit x={A_x,B_x}.

Keep only pairs contained in W. Their number m is |W|/2<=|B|/2.

The symmetric seed T gives complete bipartite adjacency between any two orbit
pairs whenever it gives any adjacency at all. Indeed changing either endpoint
to its negative interchanges a sum with a difference, and T=-T preserves the
membership condition. Define Q on the m surviving orbits by these adjacencies.
Thus a Q-edge lifts to K2,2.

The single seed s gives another quotient graph R, described in the next section.
If an edge belongs to both Q and R, retain it in Q and remove it from R; this does
not affect which supports may be counted. The red graph remains triangle-free.

7. The single-seed red graph and the injection
---------------------------------------------

ZERO-LAYER CASE. Suppose s=(0,a), where a is nonzero. Then R is the induced
subgraph, on surviving orbit indices, of the Cayley graph with differences +/-a.
Since a has order five, the latter is a disjoint union of C5's.

For y=x+a, the single-seed link between orbit x and orbit y has exactly the
three edges

  A_x A_y,   B_x B_y,   B_x A_y,

and its missing cross pair is A_x B_y. If both orbit indices belong to the
support of an independent set in the lifted link, their selected copies are
therefore uniquely forced to be A_x and B_y. At an index y, simultaneous selected
neighbours y-a and y+a would force both B_y and A_y respectively, a contradiction.
Thus the red subgraph induced on the support has maximum degree at most one.

NONZERO-LAYER CASE. Negating the entire group if necessary, assume s=(1,a).
The red quotient is the matching given by y=-a-x. Its single fixed index
x=-a/2=2a corresponds to the pair {2s,-2s}, which has already been deleted as
looped. For two distinct matched indices x,y, the single-seed link consists of

  A_x B_y,   B_x A_y,   B_x B_y,

with A_x A_y missing. Thus if both indices are selected, the selected copies
are uniquely forced to be A_x and A_y. Again R is triangle-free and its induced
degree condition is automatic because R is a matching.

Now let I be a maximal independent set of the looped link, regarded as a maximal
independent set of the simple induced graph on W. Define its support D to be the
set of orbit indices at which I selects at least one copy.

* D is independent in Q, since a Q-edge lifts to all four possible edges.
* R[D] has maximum degree at most one, by the previous descriptions.
* D dominates Q union R. Otherwise an orbit outside D with no quotient neighbour
  in D would have no selected neighbour at either copy, contradicting maximality.

Moreover, the support determines I uniquely. For a red edge of R[D], the selected
copies at both ends were just shown to be forced. If an index of D has no red
neighbour in D, it has no selected neighbour in any other orbit, since D is
Q-independent. There is no internal orbit edge. Maximality therefore forces
both copies in that orbit to be selected. These rules specify every selected
copy, proving injectivity.

Notice that no converse is needed: some colored dominating dissociation sets
may fail to give maximality at an outside orbit, which only improves the bound.

8. Uniform exact corollary
--------------------------

Applying Theorem (1.1) to the support injection gives

  mis(L_S[B]) <= C(Q,R) <= 3^(m/2)
              =3^(|W|/4) <=3^(|B|/4).            (8.1)

If s lies in a nonzero layer, at least the orbit {2s,-2s} was deleted, so in fact

  mis(L_S[B]) <= 3^((|B|-2)/4).                  (8.2)

These inequalities hold for arbitrary sizes and ranks of T, including symmetric
seeds meeting all three external layers. There is no fixed-rank restriction and
no additive O(|K|) core error. For G0=F5^k, |B|=2*5^k/5, (8.1) is the desired
local exponent 3^(5^k/10) throughout this infinite family.

The remaining general proof problem still includes seeds with at least two
unmatched antipodal elements. The present result must not be reported as a
proof of all such seeds or of the original counting conjecture.
