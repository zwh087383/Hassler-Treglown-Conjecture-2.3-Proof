TWO UNPAIRED ELEMENTS IN OPPOSITE NONZERO LAYERS
==============================================
Status: complete paper proof using the colored dominating-dissociation
lemma in round4/colored_dd_proof.txt. The reduction below was independently
checked by round4_zero after being proposed by root.

Setup. Let G=F_5 x H, where H is a finite vector space over F_5. Put
h=|H|, n=5h, and B={2,3} x H. Let S subset G\B be sum-free and suppose
    S=T union {s,t},   T=-T,
    s=(1,b),   t=(4,c),   t != -s.
For the intended two-defect case s,t are outside T. The proof below does
not otherwise restrict the size, rank, or layer distribution of T.
The condition t != -s is equivalent to c != -b.

Theorem.
    mis(L_S[B]) <= 3^((h-3)/2) <= 3^(h/2)=3^(n/10).
In particular every external sum-free seed whose exactly two unpaired
elements lie one each in layers 1 and 4 satisfies the target bound.

1. All forbidden vertices are deleted in antipodal pairs.
The link-graph loop set is
    Lambda=B intersect ((S+S) union (S-S) union (1/2)S).
Because T=-T, the terms involving T give the symmetric sets
    T+T, (1/2)T, +/-(s+T), +/-(t+T).
The remaining terms that can meet B are
    {2s,-2s}, {2t,-2t}, {s-t,t-s},
where (1/2)s=3s=-2s and (1/2)t=3t=-2t in characteristic five.
The cross sum s+t has first coordinate zero and does not meet B; the
zero arising from a self-difference is also outside B. Consequently
    Lambda=-Lambda.

As usual define antipodal fibers
    C_x={X_x,Y_x},  X_x=(2,x),  Y_x=(3,-x),  x in H.
Each is entirely legal or entirely deleted. Let V subset H index the
legal fibers.

There are at least three distinct deleted fibers:
    C_{2b},   C_{3c},   C_{b-c}.
Indeed their two points are respectively {2s,s/2}, {t/2,2t}, and
{s-t,t-s}. These three indices are pairwise distinct. The equalities
2b=3c, 2b=b-c, or 3c=b-c each imply c=-b in F_5, contradicting the
hypothesis. Thus
    |V| <= h-3.                                                (1)
The hypotheses force h>=5, so this bound causes no small-order exception.

2. The symmetric part gives uniform complete fiber adjacency.
On distinct legal fibers, the T-generated link edges are all four edges
or none: replacing a vertex z by -z exchanges sums and differences,
while T=-T removes the sign. There are no edges inside a legal fiber,
since an edge between z and -z would force 2z in T, making it a loop
vertex. Let Q be the simple graph on V recording the complete pairs.

3. The two defects give two differently constrained matchings.
For s=(1,b), distinct fibers C_x,C_y interact exactly when
    x+y=-b.
The three s-generated edges between them are
    X_x Y_y,   Y_x X_y,   Y_x Y_y.
The unique compatible pair across these occupied fibers is therefore
    X_x, X_y.                                                  (2)
The map x -> -b-x is an involution. Its unique fixed point is x=2b,
whose fiber was deleted. Hence these edges define a matching M_s on V.

For t=(4,c), distinct fibers interact exactly when
    x+y=c.
The three t-generated edges between them are
    X_x X_y,   X_x Y_y,   Y_x X_y.
Their unique compatible pair is
    Y_x, Y_y.                                                  (3)
The involution x -> c-x has the unique fixed point x=3c, already deleted,
so these edges define a second matching M_t on V.

Since c != -b, M_s and M_t share no edge. More generally, two such
three-edge patterns with different missing pairs would combine to a
complete K_{2,2}, which belongs in Q. Remove from M_s union M_t all edges
already in Q, and call the remaining graph R, retaining the labels s,t.
Each vertex has at most one incident edge of either label. Thus R has
maximum degree at most two, and all its cycles alternate the two labels.
In particular R is triangle-free.

The legal original graph is now completely described by Q and R:
Q-edges give complete fiber adjacency, and each R-edge has the three
edges described in (2) or (3). There are no other edges.

4. Exact support correspondence.
For a maximal independent set I in the legal graph, set
    U={x in V: I intersects C_x}.
Then U is independent in Q. If a vertex of R[U] had degree two, one
incident edge would have label s and the other label t. The first would
force its selected fiber to contain only X, by (2), and the second only
Y, by (3). This is impossible. Hence
    Delta(R[U]) <= 1.                                         (4)

An isolated vertex of R[U] has no occupied R-neighbor and, by Q-
independence, no occupied Q-neighbor. Both vertices of its fiber are
compatible with all outside selections. Maximality therefore forces
both to be selected. On a selected R-edge the singleton choices are
uniquely prescribed by (2) or (3). Therefore U uniquely determines I.
An empty fiber with no occupied neighbor in Q union R has undominated
vertices. Thus U dominates Q union R.

These statements already give the injection needed for the upper bound.
In fact the converse also holds. Given a Q-independent U satisfying
(4) and dominating Q union R, select both fiber vertices at isolated
vertices of R[U]; use the X,X pair on each label-s edge and the Y,Y pair
on each label-t edge. This is independent. Occupied fibers are internally
dominated, because a missing partner on an occupied edge is adjacent
to the selected vertex at its other end.

Consider an empty fiber C_x. An occupied Q-neighbor dominates it in full.
Otherwise choose an occupied R-neighbor y. If y is isolated in R[U],
its full fiber is selected and dominates C_x. If y is paired in R[U],
its other R-edge has the label opposite to xy, since there is at most
one incident edge of each label. When xy has label s, the selected
vertex at y is consequently Y_y, which is adjacent to both X_x and Y_x
by the three-edge pattern in Section 3. When xy has label t, the selected
vertex at y is X_y and likewise dominates both vertices of C_x. Every
empty fiber is therefore dominated. This proves maximality and the
exact support correspondence.

5. Counting.
The colored dominating-dissociation lemma applies because Q is arbitrary
and R is triangle-free. It bounds the number of supports by 3^(|V|/2).
Using (1),
    mis(L_S[B]) = F(Q,R) <= 3^(|V|/2) <= 3^((h-3)/2).
This completes the proof. QED.

Scope and limitations.
The theorem adds arbitrary symmetric backgrounds T to the previously
known two-element opposite-layer pattern. It is not limited to T in
the zero layer and introduces no exceptional core factor. It does not
handle two unpaired elements both in layer 1 (or both in layer 4), nor
a zero-layer unpaired element together with a nonzero-layer one. In
those cases the two local compatibility patterns need not force the
same support-degree restriction. When t=-s the whole seed is symmetric
and the earlier, stronger symmetric theorem applies instead.
