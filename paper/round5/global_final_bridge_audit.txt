Notation. G=F_5^k, n=5^k, H=F_5^(k-1), h=|H|, and
B={2,3} x H. Thus |B|=N=2h=2n/5. A sum-free set has no solution x+y=z,
including repeated entries. For disjoint sum-free sets B,S, the looped link
L_S[B] has ordinary edges xy when x+y is in S or x-y is in S or y-x is in S;
it has a loop at x precisely when S union {x} is not sum-free. Independent
sets in this graph are exactly those I subset B for which S union I is
sum-free. Write mis for the number of inclusion-maximal independent sets.

1. Elementary monotonicity used in this proof
--------------------------------------------

If J is an induced subgraph of a simple finite graph F, then mis(J)<=mis(F).
Indeed, extend each maximal independent set I of J to a maximal independent
set E(I) of F, choosing a deterministic extension. Since I is already maximal
inside J, E(I) intersect V(J)=I. Therefore this extension map is injective.

For a looped graph L, let F be its ordinary-edge graph on all vertices and let
U be the vertices without loops. Then mis(L)=mis(F[U])<=mis(F). This is the
only monotonicity needed when loops are suppressed. No claim about arbitrary
edge additions is made or used.

2. Short large-degree fingerprint lemma
--------------------------------------

LEMMA. Every simple graph F on N vertices satisfying delta(F)>=4096 and
Delta(F)<=2 delta(F) satisfies

  log_3 mis(F)/N
    < 8192/35331 + 8/513
     = 1495048/6041601
     = 1/4 - 61409/24166404.                       (1)

Proof. Fix an ordering of V(F). For each maximal independent set I construct
T subset I, initially empty, with A=V(F)\N_F(T). While some v in I has at
least 512 neighbours in F[A], add the first such v to T and recompute A.
Every selected vertex deletes at least 512 previously undeleted neighbours.
Since T is independent, T is disjoint from N_F(T), and hence

  513|T| <= N.

At termination put U={v in A : deg_{F[A]}(v)<=511}. The construction guarantees
I subset U. Moreover, I is maximal independent in F[U], since it was maximal
in F. The fingerprint T determines A and U, so each T accounts for at most
mis(F[U]) possible sets I.

Write delta=delta(F), Delta=Delta(F). Every vertex of U has at least
delta-511 neighbours in N_F(T). Counting edges from U into N_F(T) gives

  (delta-511)|U| <= Delta|N_F(T)| <= Delta(N-|U|).

Consequently

  |U| <= Delta N/(delta+Delta-511)
       <= 2delta N/(3delta-511)
       <= 8192 N/11777.

The first rational expression increases with Delta because delta>511; the
second decreases with delta. This justifies both substitutions. Moon--Moser
therefore gives mis(F[U])<=3^(8192N/35331).

The standard binomial entropy estimate, with p=1/513, yields

  sum_{j<=N/513} binom(N,j)
    <= exp(N[-p ln p-(1-p)ln(1-p)])
    <= exp(N(ln513+1)/513)
     < 3^(8N/513).

Here -(1-p)ln(1-p)<=p. Also e>8/3, since the terms through 1/3! sum to 8/3
and later terms are positive, and (8/3)^7>513 by 8^7>513*3^7. Thus ln513<7.
Finally e<3 follows from j!>=2^(j-1) for j>=2, with strict inequality for
j>=3; hence ln3>1. The last strict inequality is fully elementary.
Multiplying the two bounds proves (1). QED.

3. Application to every sufficiently large seed
-----------------------------------------------

Let S subset G\B be sum-free. Put A=S_0, P=S_1, Q=-S_4, where S_i is the
tail set in first-coordinate layer i. Set

  d=|A union (-A)|+|P union Q|.

The ordinary difference-edge graph on B is d-regular. Within each layer its
neighbours have tail displacement in A union (-A); between the two layers
the possible displacements are exactly P union Q (with the sign reversed
when the starting layer is reversed). These two contributions are disjoint.
There are no difference loops, because the zero element is absent from S.

At a vertex in layer 2 the extra sum-edge degree is at most |A|+|Q|; at a
vertex in layer 3 it is at most |A|+|P|. These are both at most d, even if
some sum edges coincide with difference edges or would form loops.
Thus, for the ordinary graph F retaining all vertices of B,

  delta(F)>=d>=|S|/2,   Delta(F)<=2d<=2delta(F).

When |S|>=8192, the lemma applies. By the induced-subgraph injection in
Section 1,

  mis(L_S[B]) <= mis(F) < 3^(N/4).                 (2)

This argument does not need a rank bound or a hypothesis |S|=o(n).

4. Bounded seeds and the exceptional core: uniform error
-------------------------------------------------------

PROVED LOCAL THEOREM (GB). For every finite-dimensional F_5-vector space K and every
sum-free S subset {0,1,4} x K, its generic link block, consisting of two
opposite nonzero residual cosets and the two B-layers, has at most 3^|K|
maximal independent sets. The complete proof and exhaustive dependency list
are in global_final_coverage.txt; the statement is now an established input,
not a conjectural hypothesis.

If |S|<8192, take K to be the linear span of all tail coordinates
of S in H, let r=dim K, q=|K|, and choose a complement W so H=K direct-sum W.
Then r<=|S|<=8191 and q<=5^8191.

Every link edge preserves a residual W-coordinate or negates it: difference
edges preserve it, and sum edges negate it. Every loop occurs over residual
zero. For the latter statement, each obstruction involving one B-vertex and
two S-elements expresses the B-vertex as a sum or difference of S-elements,
and so its residual coordinate is zero; an obstruction 2v in S also gives
zero residual coordinate since 2 is invertible in F_5.

It follows that L_S[B] is the disjoint union of a core on 2q vertices and
(|W|-1)/2 generic blocks on 4q vertices. The residual group W has odd order,
so zero is its only element fixed by negation. All generic blocks have the
same adjacency pattern, obtained by labelling the residual signs + and -.
This decomposition is also the odd-order case of Hassler--Treglown Lemma 2.10.

Use Moon--Moser on the core after deleting looped vertices, and (GB) on the
generic blocks. Multiplication over graph components gives

  mis(L_S[B])
    <= 3^(2q/3) (3^q)^((h/q-1)/2)
     = 3^(h/2+q/6)
    <= 3^(n/10+C),  where C=5^8191/6.             (3)

The same calculation includes W={0} and S empty. Combining (2) and (3)
gives, uniformly over every sum-free S subset G\B,

  mis(L_S[B]) <= 3^(n/10+C).                      (4)

C is one absolute constant, independent of k, S, the number of containers,
and their exceptional sets. Thus C=o(n) as k tends to infinity. No statement
of the form O_S(1)=o(n) for a varying seed is used.

5. Containers, removal, and maximal completion
---------------------------------------------

The Green--Ruzsa container theorem gives a family F_k of at most
2^[n(log n)^(-1/18)] subsets of G, covering all sum-free subsets, each
containing at most n^2(log n)^(-1/9) ordered Schur triples. A convenient
published formulation is Hassler--Treglown (2022), Lemma 2.3.

The arithmetic removal lemma gives a function rho(t)->0 as t->0 such that
a set with at most t n^2 Schur triples can be made sum-free by deleting at
most rho(t)n elements. The uniformity over the container family follows from
the same input t=(log n)^(-1/9) for every member. One exact source is Green,
Theorem 1.5, with the three sets F,F,-F: delete the union of the deleted
sets in the first two positions and the negative of the deleted set in the
third position. This removes at most three times the stated error and leaves
no solution x+y=z. The factor three is absorbed into rho.

For each F in F_k choose E_F subset F of size at most rho((log n)^(-1/9))n
such that A_F=F\E_F is sum-free. Extend A_F to a globally inclusion-maximal
sum-free set M_F; this exists because G is finite. Put E'_F=E_F\M_F.
Then M_F and E'_F are disjoint and

  F subset M_F union E'_F,   |E'_F|<=|E_F|.

Thus we have at most |F_k| pairs (M,E), with M maximal sum-free and |E|=o(n)
uniformly, whose unions cover all sum-free sets. Enlarging a container here
means covering more original sets; it is not an assertion about the number
of maximal independent sets under an edge change.

Fix one pair (M,E), and a globally maximal sum-free X subset M union E.
Set S=X intersect E and I=X intersect M. Then I is maximal independent in
L_S[M]: if a vertex of M\I could be added in the link, it could also be added
to X as a sum-free set, contradicting maximality. For each fixed S, X is
uniquely determined by I. Hence the number of such X is at most

  sum_{S subset E, S sum-free} mis(L_S[M]).        (5)

If |M|<=3n/10, deleting looped vertices and applying Moon--Moser gives
mis(L_S[M])<=3^(|M|/3)<=3^(n/10).

If |M|>3n/10, Lev's Theorem 3 applies: M subset (e+H_0) union (-e+H_0)
for a proper subgroup H_0 and e outside H_0. Its size forces
|H_0|=5^(k-1), because a proper subspace of codimension at least two gives
two cosets of total size at most 2n/25<3n/10. The union of these opposite
cosets is itself sum-free. Since M is globally maximal, equality holds:

  M=(e+H_0) union (-e+H_0).

An invertible linear map sends this set to {2,3} x F_5^(k-1): choose a
linear functional with kernel H_0 and value 2 on e, then use it as first
coordinate. The same map sends S outside B, preserves sum-freeness, and
preserves the link graph. Equation (4) therefore bounds every summand in
(5) by 3^(n/10+C).

Combining the two cases and summing (5) over the container family yields

  f_max(G)
    <= 2^[n(log n)^(-1/18)+rho((log n)^(-1/9))n] 3^(n/10+C)
     = 3^(n/10+o(n)).                             (6)

The o(n) is uniform. Counting a maximal sum-free set several times under
different pairs causes no issue for this upper bound.

6. Matching lower bound, with an explicit injection
--------------------------------------------------

Let s=(1,0), S={s}, B={2,3} x H. The link has loops at (2,0) and (3,0).
For each nonzero pair {x,-x} subset H it has the path

  (2,x), (3,x), (3,-x), (2,-x),

in that order, and no edges between different such paths. Each four-vertex
path has exactly three maximal independent sets. Thus

  mis(L_S[B])=3^((h-1)/2)=3^(n/10-1/2).

For every such maximal independent set I, extend S union I to a globally
maximal sum-free set X_I. This extension cannot add another B-vertex,
because I was already maximal in the link. Consequently X_I intersect B=I,
so the extension map is injective. This proves

  f_max(G)>=3^(n/10-1/2).                         (7)

Equations (6) and (7), together with the proved theorem (GB), establish

  THEOREM. f_max(F_5^k)=3^(5^k/10+o(5^k)) as k tends to infinity.

Equivalently, putting n=5^k, f_max(F_5^k)=3^(n/10+o(n)). This completes the
joint proof of the stated Hassler--Treglown asymptotic conjecture.
