EXPLICIT TWENTY-VERTEX RANK-ONE CERTIFICATES
Status: finite paper-checkable branching certificates, not merely
unverified numerical MIS counts. The accompanying Python file builds
each graph twice, once from the generic formulas and once directly
from the original F_5^3 link conditions, and then checks the stated
depth-six branch histograms using integer arithmetic only.

Vertex order: (sign,row,x), sign=0,1, row=X,Y, x=0,1,2,3,4, in that
lexicographic order. sign 0 represents +g, sign 1 represents -g, with
K=span(a) and g independent of a. There are twenty vertices.

For a current induced graph, remove isolated vertices (which are forced
in every MIS). If the depth budget is zero, apply the Moon--Moser
integer bound
  M(0)=M(1)=1;
  M(n)=3^(n/3) for n=0 mod3;
       4*3^((n-4)/3) for n=1 mod3, n>=4;
       2*3^((n-2)/3) for n=2 mod3.
Otherwise choose a maximum-degree vertex, breaking ties by the smallest
vertex index, and use
  m(F)<=m(F-v)+m(F-N[v]).
Recurse to depth six. Empty graphs terminate immediately with count1.

Every upper bound is below 3^5=243, the target for twenty vertices.
The degree sets are respectively {8,6},{8,6},{7,6},{7,6},{8,7},{8}.
All listed seeds are sum-free. No exhaustive enumeration of all
independent sets is used by this certificate.

Why these are the only dependent parameters:
- A={+/-a},P={0,r},Q={} requires r!=0,+/-a, leaving r=2a,3a.
- A={a},P={0,r},Q={0} has the same possibilities.
- A={a},P={0,r},Q={a} additionally forbids r=2a because r-a=a,
  leaving r=3a.
- A={a},P={0,r},Q={0,r+a} forbids r=+/-a and r=-2a, leaving r=2a.
Thus these certificates complete the rank-one cases of the four
independent-rank models listed in global_mixed_size4_table.txt.

branching rule also give the following five certificates. Depth is six

For the same-layer triple, normalization P={0,r} and A={1} excludes
r=0,+/-1, leaving r=2,3. For the opposite-layer triple normalize P={0}
and Q={q}. The value q=0 gives a symmetric cross core plus one zero
seed; q=4 violates sum-freeness because -q=1 is in A. This leaves
q=1,2,3 as displayed. Two zero seeds on a line, if sum-free, must be
opposite; together with one nonzero-layer seed they are covered by the
previous symmetric-core-plus-one theorem, so no further rank-one
triple occurs.
