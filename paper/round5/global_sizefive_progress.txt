ROUND 5: SIZE-FIVE PROGRESS, INCLUDING THE NINE-REGULAR SINGLE-P4 CASE
Status: complete size-five classification. The lemmas below, together
with the two completion references at the end, cover every mixed seed
of size five. Exact powers are checked by global_sizefive_certificates.py.

Use the generic-component coordinates and notation of
global_small_seed_potential.txt. In particular X_x,Y_x lie over the two
cosets +/-g+K, translation edges preserve coset sign, and reflection
edges reverse it. Put c=log_2(3)/3.

1. GENERALIZED NESTED TRIANGLE-FREE OBSERVATION
If the pure-zero translation subgraph for A is triangle-free and
(P union Q)-(P union Q) avoids A union -A, then the entire generic
component is triangle-free. A triangle has zero or two reflection
edges. The first possibility lies in the zero subgraph; the second
forces a forbidden difference of reflection labels.
For a sum-free A of size two, the zero subgraph is triangle-free.
Indeed, a same-row triangle with mixed signs would give a forbidden
sum within A; an all-equal-sign relation would require 2a+b=0 or
2b+a=0 (or 3a=0), and in characteristic five the first two imply
2b=a or 2a=b, again forbidden. Mixed-row triangles likewise give a
forbidden sum in A. The same argument applies to a size-three A
containing an opposite pair: an all-positive zero sum cannot use
three distinct elements, and repetitions are ruled out as just above.
If P,Q are nested, the required difference avoidance follows from
sum-freeness in the larger layer set.

2. TRIANGLE-FREE 9/6 AND 9/7 DEGREE-CAP LEMMA
Suppose a triangle-free graph has equally many high vertices of degree
9 and low vertices of a fixed degree l in {6,7}. Suppose every high
vertex has at least four low neighbors, hence at most five high
neighbors. Then
    m(G)<=2^{(16n-e)/31}<3^{n/4}.
Use the fixed potential mu=(16n-e)/31. In any induced subgraph of
maximum degree at most eight, each degree-eight vertex must be high
and must retain at least three low neighbors of degree at most seven.
Therefore the already established class-C8 theorem applies (the later
extension to every triangle-free maximum-degree-eight graph also
suffices). If the current maximum degree is nine, choose a degree-nine
vertex. Its neighborhood meets at most 5*9+4*7=73 edges. The two drops
are at least 7/31 and 87/31, whose terms are respectively less than
107/125 and 18/125. This closes induction. Initially e/n=(9+l)/4;
the worst exponent is l=6, namely 49/124, and 2^49<3^31 proves the
strict comparison. This includes both the size-four 9/6 and the
size-five nested 9/7 generic families.

3. SINGLETON-ZERO, DISJOINT TWO-BY-TWO LAYERS: ALL NINE-REGULAR CASES
Assume A={t}, P={p,r}, Q={q,s}, and P intersect Q is empty. The generic
component is nine-regular. Seed sum-freeness forbids differences +/-t
within P or within Q, and forbids p'-q'=t for p' in P,q' in Q. Thus
every +/-t adjacency within U=P union Q has the orientation q'=p'+t.
There are zero, one, or two such pairs, and they form a matching.

If there are no pairs, the generic component is triangle-free and is
covered by the triangle-free maximum-degree-ten theorem.
For the remaining cases fix
    beta=c/14, mu=(c+beta)n-beta e.
Since the original graph is nine-regular, mu/n=3c/4 exactly. We prove
the potential bound for every induced subgraph of this fixed original
component, using induction on the number of surviving vertices.

3a. Residual maximum degree at most eight.
The general master lemma applies with D=8 and the same beta. Its high
endpoint terms are
    3^(-1/6)<5/6, 3^(-71/42)<1/6.
Also beta<1/25. Hence all such residual graphs satisfy the required
potential bound, without any inherited regularity assumption.

3b. Two cross pairs.
Each cross pair q'=p'+t supplies three different edges inside every
vertex neighborhood. For a Y-center y, these are
    {Y_{y+t},X_{-y-q'}},
    {X_{-y-q'},X_{-y-p'}},
    {X_{-y-p'},Y_{y-t}}.
For an X-center the corresponding three edges have the rows reversed
and the signs reversed in the natural way. Different pairs supply
different edges. Thus every original neighborhood has at least six
edges. Any degree-nine vertex in an induced subgraph retains its whole
original neighborhood and those six edges. Its closed neighborhood
meets at most 81-6=75 edges. The branch terms are
    3^(-1/7)<6/7, 3^(-25/14)<1/7.
Together with 3a this completes this case.

3c. Exactly one cross pair, say q=p+t.
Each original neighborhood contains the three-edge P4 displayed in 3b.
Take any degree-nine vertex v in a residual graph F. If at least two
of its neighbors have current degree at most eight, its closed
neighborhood meets at most 81-2-3=76 edges. The branch terms are
    3^(-1/7)<171/200,
    3^(-37/21)<29/200,
so their sum is strictly less than one.

Suppose instead that at most one neighbor of v has degree at most eight.
Let w be its same-coordinate opposite-row partner. They are nonadjacent.
Their original common neighborhood has exactly six vertices. For
v=X_x,w=Y_x those six are
    X_{x+t}, Y_{x-t},
    Y_{-x-p},Y_{-x-r},X_{-x-q},X_{-x-s}.
All six survive since v retains degree nine. If w were absent, all six
would have lost an original neighbor, contradicting the assumption.
Thus w survives.

Two further neighbors of w, namely
    u1=Y_{x+t}, u2=X_{-x-p},
must also survive. Indeed u1 has two neighbors in N(v),
    X_{-x-q},Y_{-x-q},
and u2 has two neighbors in N(v),
    X_{x-t},Y_{x-t}.
All four asserted incidences follow from q=p+t and the reflection-edge
labels. Removing either u_i would therefore create at least two
degree-at-most-eight vertices in N(v), a contradiction.
Hence w has degree k=8 or 9. Moreover the three neighborhood edges
    {Y_{x+t},X_{-x-q}},
    {X_{-x-q},X_{-x-p}},
    {X_{-x-p},Y_{x-t}}
all survive.

For v=Y_x,w=X_x the analogous two protected neighbors are
    X_{x-t}, Y_{-x-q}.
The first is adjacent to X_{-x-p},Y_{-x-p} in N(v); the second is
adjacent to X_{x+t},Y_{x+t} in N(v). This proves the same conclusion
in both rows. Opposite coset signs obey the same formulas.

Now exclude v, leaving F-v. The partner w still has degree k, and its
six common neighbors with v have degree at most eight. The three
neighborhood edges survive, since v and w are nonadjacent. Therefore
at most 9k-6-3=9k-9 edges meet N_{F-v}[w]. Branching at w gives the
coefficient
    C_k=3^{-(15-k)/42}+3^{-(k+4)/7}.
For k=8, bound its terms by 5/6 and 47/300; their sum is 99/100.
For k=9, bound its terms by 171/200 and 27/200; again 99/100.
Thus m(F-v)<(99/100)2^{mu(F-v)}.
In the original branch at v, the exclude term is 3^(-1/7) and the
include term is at most 3^(-12/7), because the three neighborhood
edges are retained. Consequently the total factor is less than
    (99/100)(171/200)+153/1000=19989/20000<1.
Every subsidiary branch uses strictly smaller induced subgraphs, so
this proves the induction and the full nine-regular family. QED.

4. SIZE-FIVE CLASSIFICATION AFTER THESE RESULTS
For a mixed seed |S|=5, if a0>=6 the smaller degree is at least 11,
so the general two-degree theorem applies. If a0=4 and the nonzero
layers are not nested, the smaller degree is at least 10 and the same
theorem applies. The remaining a0=4 patterns are:
  z=2, A two nonopposite vectors: nested degrees (12,9) or (10,9);
  z=3, A has an opposite pair: nested degrees (11,9) or (9,9);
  z=4, A=-A: one nonzero-layer seed, hence symmetric core plus one.
The first two lines are triangle-free by section 1, and all listed
degree pairs are already covered. The last is a prior colored-DD case.

If a0=2 and z=2, A={t,-t}; the generic graph is triangle-free for any
P,Q. With p+q=3 its degree pairs are (10,7), (8,7), (9,8). The first
and third satisfy the maximum-degree-ten/degree-sum>=17 corollary;
the middle is symmetric core plus one.

If a0=2 and z=1, p+q=4. Up to swapping layers:
  p=4,q=0: degrees (11,7), triangle-free, degree-sum 18 with max<=12;
  p=3,q=1, nested: degrees (9,7), handled by section 2;
  p=q=2, P=Q: symmetric cross core plus one zero seed;
  p=q=2, P and Q disjoint: all nine-regular cases handled by section 3.

COMPLETION OF THE LAST TWO FAMILIES.

Thus the complete mixed size-five classification is now covered.
