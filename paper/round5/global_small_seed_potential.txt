ROUND 5: COMPLETING ALL GENERIC MIXED SEEDS OF SIZE SIX AND SEVEN
Status: paper proof. Integer inequalities are checked by
global_small_seed_certificates.py. No Lean verification or novelty
claim is made. This concerns generic components; the previously proved
exceptional-core argument remains necessary in the global reduction.

0. COORDINATES AND DEGREE DATA
Write A=S_0, P=S_1, Q=-S_4, U=P union Q; put z=|A|, p=|P|, q=|Q|,
a0=|A union -A|, d0=|U|. Let K be their linear span in H. A generic
component uses coordinates x in (g+K) union (-g+K), with g not in K,
and vertices X_x=(2,x), Y_x=(3,-x).
Its edges have the following forms:
  X_x X_y: x-y in A union -A, or x+y in -Q;
  Y_x Y_y: x-y in A union -A, or x+y in -P;
  X_x Y_y: x-y in A, or x+y in -U.
Call the first (difference) type translation edges and the second
(sum) type reflection edges. Translation edges preserve the coset sign;
reflection edges exchange g+K and -g+K. All listed contributions are
distinct in a generic component.
Assume p>=q by swapping the rows if necessary. Then the Y-vertices
have degree a=a0+z+d0+p, the X-vertices degree b=a0+z+d0+q. There are
equally many of each. Every vertex has exactly h=z+d0 neighbors in the
other row. The larger degree is at most twice the smaller degree.

1. A TRIANGLE-FREE LEMMA FOR ONE ANTIPODAL ZERO DIRECTION
Suppose A is a nonempty subset of {t,-t}, t!=0, and
    (U-U) intersect {t,-t} is empty.
Then every generic component is triangle-free.
Proof: a closed triangle uses an even number of reflection edges, because
the two cosets are distinct. If it uses none, its three coordinate
increments are in {t,-t}; their sum is one of +/-t,+/-3t, never zero in
characteristic five. If it uses two reflection edges, the remaining
translation edge forces the two reflection labels to differ by t or
-t, contrary to the assumption. QED.

Two useful consequences of seed sum-freeness are:
(i) If P and Q are nested, then the displayed assumption holds. Indeed,
U is the larger one, and adding t to two seed elements in the same
nonzero layer is forbidden; this excludes both differences +/-t.
(ii) If A={t,-t}, the assumption holds without nesting. Differences
within P or Q are forbidden as in (i), and differences between P and Q
are forbidden because P-Q cannot meet A: such a pair would sum from
layers 1 and 4 into S_0.

2. TWO HEREDITARY DEGREE-CAP LEMMAS
Let c=log_2(3)/3 and mu_beta=(c+beta)n-beta e. We use the independently
proved general-graph master lemma and its subcubic proof. In each
lemma below beta is fixed once and for all, including in induced
subgraphs. Vertex types and their original degree caps are retained.

LEMMA 2A.
Let G have equally many high and low vertices, of degrees 12 and 9,
respectively. Suppose every high vertex has six low neighbors.
Then m(G)<=3^{n/4}.
Set beta=c/17. In any induced subgraph whose maximum degree is d>9,
a vertex of degree d must be high. It originally had only six high
neighbors, so at least d-6 of its surviving neighbors are low, of
current degree at most 9. All other neighbors have degree at most d.
Hence the number of edges meeting its closed neighborhood is at most
    d^2-(d-9)(d-6)=15d-54.
The two potential drops for 10<=d<=12 are consequently at least
    A=c(18-d)/17, B=c(3d+72)/17.
Their exponential terms are uniformly less than 8/9 and at most 1/9:
the first uses d<=12 and the certificate 2^51>3^32, and the second uses
d>=10, giving B>=6c=2log_2(3).
If the current maximum degree is at most 9, the general master lemma
applies with the same beta and D=9. Its endpoint terms are
    3^(-3/17)<5/6, 3^(-33/17)<1/6.
Also beta<1/25. Thus induction proves m<=2^mu for the entire hereditary
class. Initially e/n=(12+9)/4, so mu/n=3c/4, giving the claim.

LEMMA 2B.
Let G have equally many high and low vertices, of degrees 10 and 9.
Suppose each high vertex has five low neighbors and at least two edges
inside its open neighborhood. Then m(G)<=3^{n/4}.
Set beta=c/15<1/25. An induced subgraph's degree-10 vertex must be high
and must retain its entire original neighborhood, including its five
low neighbors and the two neighborhood edges. Thus the number of
edges meeting its closed neighborhood is at most
    5*10+5*9-2=93.
The exclude and include drops are at least 2c/5 and 83c/15; the two
terms are less than 13/15 and 2/15. In any residual graph of maximum
degree at most 9, the master lemma with the fixed beta and D=9 applies:
its endpoint terms are 3^(-7/45)<17/20 and 3^(-79/45)<3/20.
This proves m<=2^mu, and the original average degree again makes
mu/n=3c/4. QED.

3. A UNIFORM LOCAL TRIANGLE DICHOTOMY WHEN A={t}
If U-U avoids +/-t, lemma 1 gives triangle-freeness. Otherwise choose
u,v in U with v=u+t (reverse their order if necessary). For every
high vertex Y_y, the following two edges lie inside its neighborhood:
    {Y_{y+t}, X_{-y-v}},  {Y_{y-t}, X_{-y-u}}.
The edges from Y_y to the Y-vertices are translation edges; those to the
X-vertices and the two displayed edges are reflection edges with labels
u or v. The displayed edges are distinct, since t!=-t and u!=v.
Thus every high vertex has at least two neighborhood edges. This
observation is valid on both coset signs and in every generic component.

4. ALL MIXED SEEDS OF SIZE SEVEN
Here z>=1. If a0>=4, then b>=a0+|S|>=11 and theorem C of
global_general_potential_audit.txt applies. Otherwise a0=2, so A is a
nonempty subset of {t,-t}. If d0>max(p,q), again b>=10 and theorem C
applies. Thus only nested P,Q need consideration, and lemma 1 makes
their generic components triangle-free.
For z=1, p+q=6, the unordered degree pairs are
    (15,9), (13,9), (11,9), (9,9).
The first two use the D>=12 triangle-free potential, the third the
maximum-degree-12/degree-sum>=18 corollary. The last has P=Q, so S is
a symmetric core plus a single zero-layer element, already covered by
the colored dominating-dissociation theorem. (It is also triangle-free
and covered by the maximum-degree-10/degree-sum>=17 corollary.)
For z=2, p+q=5, the pairs are
    (14,9), (12,9), (10,9),
all covered by the triangle-free potential corollaries.
Thus every generic component from a mixed seed of size seven meets
the required bound.

5. ALL MIXED SEEDS OF SIZE SIX
Again a0>=4 implies b>=10, so theorem C applies. Assume a0=2.
If d0>=max(p,q)+2, the same b>=10 argument applies.

For z=2, A={t,-t}. Lemma 1 gives triangle-freeness regardless of nesting.
If p+q=4 and d0-max(p,q) is zero or one, the possible degree pairs are
    (12,8), (10,8), (8,8), (11,9), (9,9).
All except (8,8) are covered by the triangle-free corollaries. In (8,8)
one has P=Q and A=-A, so the entire seed is symmetric, already proved.

For z=1, p+q=5. If P,Q are nested, lemma 1 gives triangle-freeness and
the pairs are (13,8), (11,8), (9,8), all covered by those corollaries.
The remaining nonnested case has d0=max(p,q)+1. It has either
    p=4,q=1,d0=5: (a,b,h)=(12,9,6),
or p=3,q=2,d0=4: (a,b,h)=(10,9,5).
The first uses lemma 2A. For the second, the dichotomy in section 3
gives either a triangle-free graph (handled by the degree-10 theorem)
or the neighborhood-edge hypothesis of lemma 2B. Both meet the target.
This exhausts mixed seeds of size six.

CONCLUSION.
Combined with theorem C for |S|>=8, every generic component of every
mixed seed with |S|>=6 satisfies m<=3^{n/4}. Pure-zero seeds are handled
in full by zero_pure_layer_theorem.txt. The smaller mixed sizes are now
covered in global_zero_layer_generic_theorem.txt, and all no-zero seeds
in matrix_nozero_remaining.txt with the final entropy proofs. The
completed joint theorem and its global bridge are assembled in
