1. The strip bijections use all 100 vertices, with exactly two phases
of ten-vertex slices and only neighboring-slice interfaces. The row
coordinate r=2x_1+1-(row mod2), h=floor(row/2), j=x_0 is bijective.
The verifier constructs edges directly from y+z,y-z,z-y in the actual
F_5^4 seed, independently constructs the strip, and compares every
adjacency list. It also checks seed sum-freeness and that all generic
vertices avoid the full loop set. All these checks passed.

2. Internal independence plus compatibility across adjacent interfaces
is exactly independence in the original graph. For consecutive selected
slice sets L,S, the deficit U=D_0(S) minus previous-slice domination is
uniquely determined. The transition requirement U subset In(T) ensures
that the next slice dominates every remaining unselected vertex.
Conversely every closed walk obeying the stated transition conditions
selects an independent dominating set. Thus closed walks and maximal
independent sets are in bijection, not merely bounded in one direction.
The feasibility pruning does not delete any actual closed walk, since
its actual previous and next slice sets witness the existential tests.

3. A fixed starting slice prevents cyclic overcounting. The alternating
two-step matrix B records exactly the intermediate states, and trace B^5
counts each MIS once. Different deficit states cannot create duplicate
counts for the same selected sets because each deficit is fixed by the
actual previous and current selected sets.

4. All matrix entries are nonnegative integers. The proposed vector w
has positive entries and satisfies Bw<=lambda w entrywise. Hence
B^5w<=lambda^5w, and each diagonal entry of B^5 is at most lambda^5.
Therefore trace B^5<=dim(B)*lambda^5, with no spectral approximation.
The simultaneous j-rotation is a graph symmetry, and the verifiers
check every state orbit, all orbit sizes, and the constancy of weights
and their products. Thus the short orbit tables cover all rows.
