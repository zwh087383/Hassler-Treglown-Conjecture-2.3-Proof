ROUND 5: TWO TRIANGLE-FREE POTENTIAL THEOREMS AND THE NO-ZERO CUTOFF
Status: proved on paper, independently derived; exact numerical
certificates are in global_trianglefree_certificates.py.
No Lean formalization or novelty claim is made.

All graphs are finite and simple. Write m(G) for the number of maximal
independent sets, n for vertices and e for edges. Define
    nu_b(G)=n/2-b(e-n/2)=(1+b)n/2-be.
For a fixed b, this is additive over components. If v has maximum degree
d in a triangle-free graph, ordinary include/exclude branching gives
potential drops
    A_b(d)=1/2-b(d-1/2),
    B_b(d)=(d+1)/2-b(d^2-(d+1)/2).
Indeed, the neighborhood of v is independent, and at most d^2 edges
meet N[v]. The sum of the two exponential branch terms is convex as a
function of real d: the exponents are respectively affine and convex
quadratic.

THEOREM 1 (maximum degree at most 10).
For every triangle-free graph G of maximum degree at most 10,
    m(G)<=2^{nu_{1/36}(G)}.

PROOF OF THEOREM 1.
Induct on n, keeping b=1/36 fixed in all induced subgraphs. Isolated
vertices are harmless, and an isolated K2 has potential exactly 1 and
count 2. When the current maximum degree d is at least 4, convexity
reduces to d=4,10. The respective drops are
    d=4: A=29/72, B=17/8;
    d=10: A=17/72, B=23/8.
The branch terms are respectively less than 19/25,6/25 and 17/20,3/20.
Thus both sums are strictly below 1.

For current maximum degree at most three we use stronger local bounds.

(i) Non-isolated leaf. A leaf v with neighbor w of degree 2 or 3 gives
    m(G)=m(G-{v,w})+m(G-N[w]).
For degree 2 the drops are at least 35/36 and 103/72, whose branch terms
are less than 51/100 and 3/8. Their sum is 177/200.
For degree 3 the drops are at least 17/18 and 67/36; the terms are less
than 21/40 and 1/3, whose sum is less than 177/200.
Hence a subcubic graph containing a non-isolated leaf satisfies
    m(G)<(177/200)2^{nu(G)}.

(ii) Minimum degree two and a 2--3 edge. Choose a vertex w of degree 3
adjacent to v of degree 2. If u is v's other neighbor, triangle-freeness
ensures wu is absent. Thus G-w has the non-isolated leaf v, with u still
of degree at least two. Apply (i) to the exclude-w branch. The drops
are 31/72 and at least 11/6, since w's neighbor-degree sum is at most 8.
The exponential factors are less than 3/4 and 9/32. The total factor is
    (177/200)(3/4)+9/32=189/200.

(iii) Cubic graph. In a connected cubic triangle-free graph, deleting a
vertex leaves a 2--3 edge: a neighbor y of the deleted vertex has degree
2, and either of its other neighbors still has degree 3 by the absence
of triangles. The delete-vertex and include-vertex drops are 31/72 and
65/36. The factors are less than 3/4 and 29/100, respectively. Using (ii),
    (189/200)(3/4)+29/100=799/800<1.

(iv) Cycles. A connected 2-regular triangle-free graph is C_k, k>=4.
If a_k=m(C_k), then a_4=2,a_5=5,a_6=5, and
    a_k=a_{k-2}+a_{k-3} (k>=7).
For completeness, encode a cyclic independent dominating set by states
0,1,2 according to the number of unselected vertices since the last
selected vertex. The allowed transition matrix is
    T=[[0,1,0],[1,0,1],[1,0,0]].
For k>=3 there is a bijection between such cyclic words and closed
state walks, so a_k=tr(T^k). Direct multiplication gives T^3=T+I,
proving the recurrence. Since (7/5)^(-2)+(7/5)^(-3)=300/343<1 and the
three initial counts are below (7/5)^k, induction gives a_k<=(7/5)^k.
The exact certificate 7^72<5^72*2^35 gives
    (7/5)^k<2^{35k/72}=2^{nu_{1/36}(C_k)}.
These cases exhaust the subcubic components. Theorem 1 is proved.

THEOREM 2 (maximum degree at most D, D>=12).
For every triangle-free graph G with maximum degree at most D,
    m(G)<=2^{nu_b(G)}, b=2/[7(D-1)].

PROOF FOR D>=13.
Here b<=1/42<1/36. If the current maximum degree is at most 10, remove
isolated vertices and apply theorem 1 to the remaining graph H. Since
e(H)>=|V(H)|/2, reducing b increases the potential. Add back the positive
potential of isolated vertices. This proves the needed bound in this
case. For current degrees at least 4 (in particular 11 or larger), use
convexity and the endpoints 4,D.
At 4 the drops are at least 5/12 and 61/28>2. Their terms are less than
3/4 and 1/4, because 3^12>2^19.
At D the drops equal
    A=(3D-5)/[14(D-1)], B=(3D+5)/14.
They increase with D. At D=13, A=17/84>1/5 and B=22/7>3. The first term
is less than 7/8 because 2*7^5>8^5, and the second is less than 1/8.
This closes the induction for every D>=13.

PROOF FOR D=12.
Fix b=2/77<1/36 and induct on n. Current maximum degree at most 10 is
covered by theorem 1 exactly as above. For current maximum degree 11,
the drops are A=5/22>1/5, B=232/77>3, so the terms are less than 7/8,1/8.

For a degree-12 vertex put
    x=2^(-31/154), y=2^(-41/14),
    z1=2^(-65/22), z2=2^(-459/154).
Integer certificates give
    x<87/100, y<33/250, z1<13/100, z2<127/1000.
If a degree-12 vertex has a neighbor of degree at most 11, its closed
neighborhood meets at most 143 edges. Its branch coefficient is at most
    x+z1<1.
If that neighbor has degree at most 10, the coefficient is at most
    x+z2<997/1000.
These strengthened inequalities follow by branching at that degree-12
vertex and applying the inductive hypothesis to proper induced graphs.

If a component containing degree-12 vertices is not 12-regular, a path
from a degree-12 vertex to a smaller-degree vertex contains a 12--<=11
edge, giving the preceding bound. It remains to handle a 12-regular
component C. Delete any v in C and call the graph H. Each original
neighbor u of v now has degree 11, and every neighbor of u in H has
degree 12: such a neighbor cannot have been adjacent to v, by
triangle-freeness. Choose one such neighbor w. In H-w, u has degree 10,
and every remaining neighbor of u still has degree 12, again because
triangles wuq are forbidden. Therefore H-w satisfies the x+z2 bound.
Branching on w in H gives the coefficient
    x(x+z2)+z1 < (87/100)(997/1000)+13/100
                =99739/100000.
Finally branch on v in C. Its coefficient is at most
    x[ x(x+z2)+z1 ]+y
        <(87/100)(99739/100000)+33/250
        =9997293/10000000<1.
The extra expansions use only proper induced subgraphs, so the induction
is well founded. Components multiply. This completes theorem 2.

TWO-DEGREE COROLLARIES.
Suppose exactly half the vertices have degree a, and half degree b0.
Then e/n=(a+b0)/4.
(1) If max(a,b0)<=10 and a+b0>=17, theorem 1 gives
    nu/n<=1/2-15/144=19/48<log_2(3)/4,
where the strict comparison is exactly 2^19<3^12.
(2) If max(a,b0)<=12 and a+b0>=18, theorem 2 with parameter D=12 gives
    nu/n<=1/2-8/77=61/154<log_2(3)/4,
where the strict comparison is exactly 2^122<3^77.
(3) If D=max(a,b0)>=12 and min(a,b0)>=D/2, theorem 2 gives
    nu/n<=11/28+1/[28(D-1)]<=61/154<log_2(3)/4.
In all three situations m(G)<3^{n/4} for nonempty graphs.

NO-ZERO-LAYER GENERIC LINK APPLICATION.
Let p=|P|,q=|Q|,d0=|P union Q|. The generic link is bipartite, hence
triangle-free, with half its vertices of degree d0+p and half d0+q.
The larger degree is at most twice the smaller degree. The previously
proved pure-layer and symmetric-plus-one cases are also available.
Every such no-zero seed S with |S|=p+q>=7 is now covered:

First suppose p+q>=8, and by symmetry assume p>=q.
- If p>=6, the maximum degree is at least 12, so corollary (3) applies.
- If p=5, then q>=3. If maximum degree is at most 12, the degree sum is
  2d0+p+q>=10+8=18 and corollary (2) applies. If larger, use (3).
- The only remaining possibility is p=q=4. If P=Q, the seed is symmetric.
  Otherwise d0>=5, so the degree sum is >=18 and (2) or (3) applies.

For p+q=7, with p>=q and both layers nonempty:
- p>=6 again uses (3).
- p=5,q=2: if d0=5, the degrees are 10 and 7, covered by (1). For
  d0>=6 the degree sum is >=19, covered by (2) or (3).
- p=4,q=3: if d0=4, Q is a subset of P and the seed consists of a
  symmetric core plus one extra element, already handled by colored-DD.
  If d0=5 or 6, the degrees have maximum <=10 and sum >=17, so (1)
  applies. If d0=7, the degrees are 11 and 10, covered by (2).
Pure-layer cases are already solved independently.

Thus every no-zero generic component arising from |S|>=7 obeys the
required 3^{n/4} bound. The smaller no-zero seeds are now completely
covered in matrix_nozero_remaining.txt, including the final rank-free
entropy arguments. The exceptional core and uniform global counting
are included in the completed joint proof in global_final_coverage.txt
and global_final_bridge_audit.txt.
