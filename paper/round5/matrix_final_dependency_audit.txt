1. COMMON BRANCHING AND EDGE ACCOUNTING

For a simple graph and a vertex v,
  mis(G)<=mis(G-v)+mis(G-N[v]).
The selected-v branch is in bijection with the MIS of G-N[v]. The
unselected-v branch injects into MIS of G-v: every other omitted vertex
must still have a selected neighbor. No monotonicity under ordinary edge
addition is used.

Put d=deg(v). The number of edges removed on deleting N[v] is exactly
  sum_{u in N(v)} deg(u)-e(G[N(v)]).
This identity counts the edges v--N(v) once, the edges inside N(v)
once after the subtraction, and edges from N(v) to the exterior once.
For a current maximum-degree vertex the expression is at most d^2.
For a triangle-free graph the subtraction term is zero. These facts
justify every initial potential-drop formula in the two files.

Both potentials are additive, while MIS counts multiply over connected
components. The empty graph and isolated vertices are handled directly.
Disconnected residual graphs cause no difficulty: either apply strong
induction to the whole proper residual graph, or multiply the bounds
on its components. A strengthened bound from one component remains a
strengthened bound for the product with the other components.

2. GENERAL-GRAPH MASTER LEMMA: FIXED BETA AND CONVEX ENDPOINTS

Here c=log_2(3)/3 and mu_beta=(c+beta)n-beta e. Fix D and beta once for
the whole induction. After deletion, beta is never recomputed from a
new maximum degree or degree profile. Every residual graph remains a
simple graph of maximum degree at most the original D.

The drops at a current maximum-degree vertex are
  A(d)=c-beta(d-1),
  B(d)=(d+1)c-beta(d^2-d-1).
The exponent -A(d) is affine in d; -B(d) has positive quadratic
coefficient beta. Thus 2^{-A(d)}+2^{-B(d)} is convex on the real interval
[4,D]. The value at every intermediate current degree is bounded by
the larger endpoint value. There is no assumption that residual graphs
remain regular or retain two degree classes.

At d=4, the uniform beta<=1/25 endpoint is justified by
  2^{-(c-3/25)}<19/25,
  2^{-(5c-11/25)}<6/25.
The stronger 3/4,1/4 bounds claimed in the narrower beta<=27/800 range
also hold. Both ranges used later really lie inside the master range.

3. GENERAL-GRAPH LOW-DEGREE CLOSURE

This is the important part that cannot simply be assumed after a
high-degree branching. I checked each residual configuration explicitly.

3a. Leaf. If v is a leaf with neighbor w of degree d=2 or 3, splitting
on whether v or w is selected gives the exact recurrence
  mis(G)=mis(G-{v,w})+mis(G-N[w]).
The first drop is 2c-beta(d-2)>=2c-beta. The second is at least
  (d+1)c-beta(2d-3),
because the neighbor-degree sum of w is at most 1+3(d-1). For d=3
this is no worse than the d=2 value 3c-beta, since c>2beta. At the worst
allowed beta=1/25, the two factors are below 1/2 and 7/20. The claimed
17/20 strengthened coefficient is therefore valid for any subcubic
graph having this configuration.

3b. A 2--3 edge. For deg(v)=2, deg(w)=3 and u the other neighbor of v,
deleting w leaves a non-isolated leaf unless deg(u)=2 and uw is an
edge. In the ordinary case the first branch receives the preceding
17/20 factor, and the drops are c-2beta and at least 4c-4beta. The
coefficient is below
  (17/20)(3/4)+13/50=359/400<19/20.

In the exceptional case u,v,w form a pendant triangle. The exact
recurrence is
  mis(G)=2mis(G-{u,v,w})+mis(G-N[w]).
Deleting the triangle meets four edges; deleting N[w] meets at most
six. The drops are at least 3c-beta and 4c-2beta, respectively. Hence
the coefficient is below 2(7/20)+1/4=19/20. This accounts for the case
where the leaf-based argument alone would not apply.

3c. Cubic graphs. In a connected cubic graph other than K4, deletion
of any v leaves minimum degree at least two and a 2--3 edge. If no
such edge existed, all three former neighbors of v would have both
remaining neighbors among those same three vertices, forcing a K4
component, hence the whole connected graph to be K4. Thus the 19/20
bound legitimately applies to G-v. The drops c-2beta and 4c-5beta give
  (19/20)(3/4)+4/15=47/48<1.
K4 itself has mu=4c-2beta>2, so its four MIS are covered directly.

3d. Exhaustion. Isolated edges have potential 2c+beta>1 and count two.
After removing leaves, a connected subcubic graph is either a cycle,+cubic, or has a 2--3 edge. For a cycle e=n and mu=cn; the ordinary
Moon--Moser inequality supplies the required bound, including C3.
Thus the low-degree proof exhausts all possibilities. The only external
standard graph theorem used in this subcase is Moon--Moser; it should
remain stated/cited or proved in the final manuscript.

All strengthened estimates are consequences of the same main strong
induction applied to their proper children. Applying a strengthened
estimate to G-v does not assume the desired stronger result for G.

4. GENERAL THEOREMS A, B, C

For beta=gamma/(D-2), the endpoint drops are
  A(D)=c-gamma-gamma/(D-2),
  B(D)=(D+1)(c-gamma)-gamma/(D-2).
Both increase with D for gamma=27/100 and 3/8, since c exceeds both
values. Thus the claimed reductions to D=10 and D=22 are sound.

At D=10 the drops are c-243/800 and 11c-2403/800, with factors below
6/7 and 1/7. At D=22 they are c-63/160 and 23c-1383/160, with factors
below 911/1000 and 89/1000. Their exact-power comparisons were
independently rechecked. The regular and two-degree exponent comparisons
reduce respectively to 3^50<2^81 and 3^160<2^261, both correct.

For theorem C, D=a and beta=c/(a+b-4) are fixed using the ORIGINAL
degrees. The master lemma applies to all residual graphs without any
two-degree hypothesis. For fixed b, x=1/(a+b-4) makes both endpoint
drops affine in x:
  A=c(b-3)x,
  B=c(b-2)-c[(b-2)(b-5)+1]x.
Convexity therefore permits checking a=b and a=2b-3. The two drops at
each endpoint increase with b>=10. Re-derivation yields the displayed
four tests at (10,10) and (17,10), with the exact powers 7/48,87/48,
7/69,143/69 of 3. The paper's four integer comparisons are correct.
Finally beta(a+b-4)/4=c/4, so the initial potential is exactly
3cn/4=(n/4)log_2(3), as asserted.

The generic-link degree calculation also checks out: with a0=|A union
-A|, z=|A|, d0=|P union Q| and p>=q, the degrees are
  a=a0+z+d0+p, b=a0+z+d0+q.
For z>=1, a0>=2, a-2b<=-3, and b>=a0+|S|>=|S|+2. Thus |S|>=8 gives
theorem C's hypotheses. This application still concerns generic
components; it does not replace the separate exceptional-core argument.

5. TRIANGLE-FREE THEOREM 1: ALL RESIDUAL DEGREES

Keep b=1/36 fixed. The potential nu_b=(1+b)n/2-be has drops
  A_b(d)=1/2-b(d-1/2),
  B_b(d)=(d+1)/2-b(d^2-(d+1)/2).
The same convexity argument applies. Direct substitution gives the
correct endpoint pairs (29/72,17/8) at d=4 and (17/72,23/8) at d=10.

The low-degree strengthened estimates are also valid:
 - A leaf with neighbor degree two has drops 35/36,103/72.
 - With neighbor degree three the drops are 17/18,67/36.
   These give the uniform factor 177/200.
 - A 2--3 edge has delete/include drops 31/72 and at least 11/6.
   Triangle-freeness ensures the leaf's other neighbor is not removed
   or degree-reduced by deleting the degree-three vertex. Hence
     (177/200)(3/4)+9/32=189/200.
 - Deleting a vertex of a cubic triangle-free component leaves a 2--3
   edge, because a common neighbor would form a triangle. The root
   drops 31/72,65/36 yield
     (189/200)(3/4)+29/100=799/800<1.

The cycle argument is correct. The stated transition matrix represents
successive gaps of two or three between selected vertices. Every closed
walk has a selected-state occurrence and determines a unique cyclic
independent dominating set. T^3=T+I gives a_k=a_{k-2}+a_{k-3}; the initial
counts 2,5,5 for C4,C5,C6 are correct. The inequalities
  (7/5)^(-2)+(7/5)^(-3)=300/343<1,
  7^72<5^72*2^35
give precisely the required cycle potential. Together these cases close
the full hereditary class of triangle-free graphs of maximum degree ten.

6. TRIANGLE-FREE THEOREM 2: SMALLER BETA AND ENDPOINTS

For D>=12 use the fixed b=2/[7(D-1)]. When a residual graph has maximum
degree at most ten, remove isolated vertices first. The remaining H has
e(H)>=|V(H)|/2, and therefore
  nu_b(H)-nu_{1/36}(H)
     =(1/36-b)(e(H)-|V(H)|/2)>=0.
Theorem 1 transfers in the correct direction. Adding back isolated
vertices only adds positive potential and does not change the count.
The removal of isolated vertices is necessary here, and the file does
explicitly do it.

For D>=13, b<=1/42. The d=4 drops are at least 5/12 and 61/28, whose
terms are below 3/4 and 1/4. At d=D the exact drops are
  (3D-5)/[14(D-1)] and (3D+5)/14.
They increase with D and at D=13 exceed 1/5 and 3, respectively. The
inequalities 3^12>2^19 and 2*7^5>8^5 justify the stated rational bounds.
Thus the convex-endpoint step covers every current degree from four
through D, including eleven and twelve when they occur in residuals.

7. THE D=12 LOOKAHEAD, CHECKED STEP BY STEP

Here b=2/77 is held fixed. Current maximum degree eleven has drops
5/22 and 232/77, giving terms below 7/8 and 1/8. Let
  x=2^(-31/154), y=2^(-41/14),
  z1=2^(-65/22), z2=2^(-459/154).

A degree-twelve vertex retaining at least one neighbor of degree at
most eleven has neighbor-degree sum at most 143, so its include drop
is 65/22 and its ordinary branch factor at most x+z1<1. A neighbor of
degree at most ten improves that sum to at most 142, giving x+z2<997/1000.
These are strengthened LOCAL configurations obtained by applying the
main induction to proper children; they do not assume the theorem for
the current graph as an extra premise.

If a connected component containing a degree-twelve vertex is irregular,
a path to a smaller-degree vertex gives the first configuration. For a
twelve-regular connected component C, the paper's three levels are:

  Level 1: choose v and put H=C-v. A neighbor u of v now has degree
  eleven. Every remaining neighbor w of u has degree twelve in H,
  since adjacency w--v would make triangle v,u,w.

  Level 2: branch at such a w in H. In H-w, u has degree ten. Every
  remaining neighbor z of u still has degree twelve: z was adjacent
  neither to v (triangle v,u,z) nor to w (triangle w,u,z). There are
  ten such remaining neighbors, so the low-neighbor configuration is
  present. The stronger x+z2 bound therefore applies to H-w.

  Level 3: its use is justified by one more branch at z in H-w, whose
  two children have fewer vertices still and use the main induction.
  The other branch H-N_H[w] at level 2 uses the ordinary inductive
  bound. Since w had the degree-eleven neighbor u in H, this gives
    mis(H)<=2^{nu(H)}[x(x+z2)+z1].

The root selected-v branch has the regular include factor y. Therefore
the overall coefficient is
  x[x(x+z2)+z1]+y.
Using the exact certified bounds
  x<87/100, y<33/250, z1<13/100, z2<127/1000
gives successively
  x+z2<997/1000,
  x(x+z2)+z1<99739/100000,
  x[x(x+z2)+z1]+y<9997293/10000000<1.

Every invocation is on a proper induced subgraph with the same b and
maximum degree at most twelve. This is a valid strong-induction proof,
not an assumption that H or H-w remains regular, and not a circular
invocation of the desired strengthened estimate.
