An 83-state transfer proof for the mixed rank-two three-seed matrix
=================================================================
Date: 2026-11-22.
Status: complete finite transfer argument with an explicit 19-row rational
arithmetic certificate. The certificate is independently evaluated by two
different formulas in matrix_rank2_transfer_verify.py. This is not a Lean
verification. It does not prove the arbitrary-matrix or all-seed conjecture.

THEOREM
-------
Let K=F_5^2 and let u,v be linearly independent. Put P={0}, Q={u,v}.
Let Gamma(P,Q) have biadjacency matrix

               [ C_(P union Q), C_Q           ]
               [ C_P,           C_(P union Q) ].

Then

  mis Gamma(P,Q)
       <= (1433053/32)^(5/2)
       < 3^25.

The same inequality per 25-element coset applies in an arbitrary ambient
F_5-vector space. Thus the desired exponent holds for every mixed pattern
with one translation in one color and two in the other. The case of
dependent normalized translations is already covered by the rank-one
result; a normalized zero translation in the other color is nested-row.

1. Coordinates and row-union map
-------------------------------
The number of MIS of a bipartite graph equals the number of different
unions of row neighborhoods. We therefore count the Boolean row span.

Use horizontal basis u-v and vertical basis v. Then the two Q translations
are (0,1) and (1,1). Write coordinates (i,j), with i,j in F_5, and use j
as the layer. For each layer j let X_j,Y_j subset F_5 be the chosen row
indices in the upper and lower row blocks, and put T_j=X_j union Y_j.

Define horizontal dilation

        delta(Z)=Z union (Z+1),    Z subset F_5.

Direct multiplication by the Boolean biadjacency matrix gives the two
output sets in layer j:

        U_j=T_j union delta(X_(j-1)),
        V_j=Y_j union delta(T_(j-1)).                 (1)

All layer indices are cyclic modulo 5. This identity is exact.

The incoming carry state in layer j is

        c_j=(p_j,q_j)=(delta(X_(j-1)),delta(T_(j-1))).

2. The carry alphabet has exactly 83 states
------------------------------------------
Let C consist of pairs (delta(X),delta(T)) with X subset T subset F_5.
Encode such a pair (p,q), p subset q, by a cyclic word w in {0,1,2}^5:
2 on p, 1 on q\p, and 0 outside q.

The word w is a carry state exactly when it has no strict local maximum:

        w_i <= max(w_(i-1),w_(i+1)) for every i.       (2)

Proof. Encode X subset T by an input word z with levels 0,1,2. The output
word of their two dilations is w_i=max(z_i,z_(i-1)), which cannot have a
strict local maximum. Conversely, when (2) holds, choose
z_i=min(w_i,w_(i+1)). Then

 max(z_i,z_(i-1))
    =min(w_i,max(w_(i-1),w_(i+1)))=w_i.

This proves the characterization.

Here is a direct count, without enumerating 243 input words. A binary
word using only 0,1 satisfies (2) precisely when its 1-runs have length
at least two. On a five-cycle there are 17: two constant words and five
rotations of a run of length 2,3,4.

If the word contains 2, its 2-positions form one cyclic run: two separate
2-runs would require at least six positions. If the run has length
5,4,3,2, respectively, the numbers of words are

             1,  5*2=10,  5*4=20,  5*7=35.

For the last count, the remaining three entries are binary, and their
middle entry cannot be 1 when both its neighbors are 0; only 010 is
forbidden among the eight binary words. Thus |C|=17+66=83.

Rotation splits C into three constant states and sixteen five-element
orbits, giving the 19 representatives in the arithmetic table below.

3. Definition of the transition matrix
--------------------------------------
For c=(p,q) and c'=(r,s) in C, define the nonnegative integer A_(c,c') as
the number of DISTINCT ordered output pairs (U,V) for which there exist
X,Y subset F_5 satisfying

 T=X union Y,
 delta(X)=r, delta(T)=s,
 U=T union p, V=Y union q.                            (3)

Distinct means that different row choices producing the same (U,V,c')
are counted only once. This detail matters for the bound.

Any complete choice of rows in the 100-vertex graph produces a closed
walk c_0,c_1,...,c_4,c_0, together with an output label on each transition.
Conversely a transition label has a witnessing choice X_j,Y_j by (3);
choosing such witnesses at the five layers is compatible because their
outgoing carries are the next layer's incoming carries.

Different full output sequences cannot be the same labeled closed walk.
An output sequence might have more than one representing walk, which
only increases the bound. Therefore

               mis Gamma(P,Q) <= tr(A^5).            (4)

4. A closed formula for each matrix entry
-----------------------------------------
The following second formula eliminates existential row choices and
gives a direct finite expression for the table's entries.

For W subset F_5 put

          epsilon(W)=W intersect (W-1).

This is the largest set Z with delta(Z) subset W. In particular,
delta(epsilon(W))=W whenever W itself is a dilation image.

For c=(p,q), c'=(r,s) and candidate outputs U,V with p subset U and
q subset V, form

          T*=U intersect epsilon(s) intersect (epsilon(r) union V).

Then (U,V) is counted in A_(c,c') if and only if all four conditions hold:

       U\p subset T*,              V\q subset T*,
       delta(T*)=s,                delta(T* intersect epsilon(r))=r. (5)

Proof of necessity. Any witnessing X,Y,T from (3) has
T subset U, T subset epsilon(s), X subset epsilon(r), and
T\epsilon(r) subset Y subset V. Thus T subset T*. The first two
inclusions in (5) follow because T must contain U\p and Y must contain
V\q. Monotonicity gives s=delta(T) subset delta(T*) subset s and
r=delta(X) subset delta(T* intersect epsilon(r)) subset r.

Proof of sufficiency. Define

       X=T* intersect epsilon(r),
       Y=(V\q) union (T*\epsilon(r)).

The two inclusions in (5) and the definition of T* give Y subset T*,
X union Y=T*, U=T* union p, and V=Y union q. The last two equalities
in (5) provide the required outgoing carry. Thus these X,Y witness (3).

Consequently every A-entry is given by a sum over only the 32^2 possible
ordered output pairs, testing the explicit bit-set conditions (5).
The verifier constructs A once from (3), once from (5), and checks that
all 83^2 entries agree.

5. Diagonal weighting and the 19-row rational certificate
--------------------------------------------------------
Let D be diagonal, with D_(c,c)=2^(|q|/2) for c=(p,q), and set B=DAD^-1.
Then tr(B^5)=tr(A^5), while

      ||B||_F^2
          =sum_(c=(p,q), c'=(r,s)) 2^(|q|-|s|) A_(c,c')^2.

Define the integral row numerator

      H(p,q)=sum_(c'=(r,s)) 2^(5+|q|-|s|) A_((p,q),c')^2.    (6)

All exponents are nonnegative. Rotation preserves (3), (5), and all
cardinalities in (6), so H is constant on each of the 19 rotation orbits.
Words are read in cyclic order; their levels encode p subset q as in
Section 2. Substitution in the explicit formula (5) gives:

Hence

             ||B||_F^2=1433053/32 <59049=3^10.         (7)

The strict comparison is simply the integer inequality

No floating-point calculation is used.

6. The final norm inequality uses only Cauchy--Schwarz
-----------------------------------------------------
For any real matrices X,Y of compatible dimensions,
||XY||_F <= ||X||_F ||Y||_F. Applying Cauchy--Schwarz to the trace and
then this submultiplicativity gives

  |tr(B^5)|
       =|sum_(i,j) (B^2)_(i,j) (B^3)_(j,i)|
       <=||B^2||_F ||B^3||_F
       <=||B||_F^5.

Combining (4) and (7),

       mis Gamma(P,Q) <= (1433053/32)^(5/2) <3^25.

This completes the finite transfer proof.

7. A smaller-check alternative to the exact row sum
---------------------------------------------------
For fixed q and p_0 subset p, both valid carry states, each transition
output under p is obtained from the corresponding output under p_0 by
replacing U with U union p. Thus

             A_((p,q),c') <= A_((p_0,q),c').

Every nonempty p contains an adjacent pair p_0, and (p_0,q) is a valid
carry whenever p_0 subset q. Consequently the largest H with p nonempty
is bounded by the seven rows with p an adjacent pair. The seven cases
give H<=18336. For p empty, the five binary orbit types give H<=39968.
There are 17 states with p empty. The fully occupied state p=q=F_5 has
A_((p,q),c')=1 for every c', and H=8928. This last value also follows
without evaluating transitions: the counts of carry states at |s|=0,2,3,4,5
are 1,10,20,35,17, giving

       H(F_5,F_5)=1024+10*256+20*128+35*64+17*32=8928.

Thus the table can alternatively be used only through the inequalities
for its five p-empty types and seven minimal nonempty-p types:

  32||B||_F^2 <=17*39968+65*18336+8928
               =1880224 <1889568=32*3^10.

This also proves the desired bound, without needing the exact total
1433053 or the other six nonconstant row evaluations.

8. Reproduction and scope
-------------------------
Run, from the workspace root:

             python3 round5/matrix_rank2_transfer_verify.py

The script uses only the Python standard library and finishes quickly.
It proves the state alphabet equivalence by direct equality of two finite
sets, constructs all matrix entries independently by (3) and (5), checks
the 19 orbit sizes and rational row values, and checks the final integer
comparison. It also supplies the supplementary exact value

                     tr(A^5)=142457592583 <3^25,

which gives a stronger numerical upper bound but is unnecessary for
the short Frobenius-norm certificate.

Files:
 matrix_rank2_transfer_verify.py -- complete small verifier;
 matrix_rank2_transfer_data.json -- all 83 states and all integer entries;
 matrix_rank2_transfer_table.txt -- the 19-row table and exact checks.

For a singleton translation p and two translations q_1,q_2, relabel both
right coordinate blocks by subtracting p. This gives P={0} and
Q={q_1-p,q_2-p}. Swapping the two row blocks and the two column blocks
interchanges P and Q, so either mixed color allocation is covered.
If the normalized translations span a two-dimensional subspace L of a
larger K, the graph decomposes into |K|/25 isomorphic components on its
cosets. Thus

       mis Gamma(P,Q) <= (1433053/32)^(|K|/10) <3^|K|.

The case of one-dimensional span is handled by the previous 212<243
paper bound (or the earlier exact rank-one enumeration). This proof
does not address arbitrary larger translation sets.
