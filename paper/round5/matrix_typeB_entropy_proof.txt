ROUND 5: A SEVEN-INPUT ENTROPY PROOF FOR THE LAST TYPE-B MATRIX

Status: complete proof with a four-row rational certificate. The script
matrix_typeB_entropy_verify.py checks all 16,384 local configurations
independently of the four-row summation, and separately checks the
rank-three graph automorphism and the seven-fold translated cover.
No floating-point optimization is used in the theorem or its certificate.

THEOREM.
Let K be a finite abelian group of size q. Take
  P={0,u}, Q={v,w},
and suppose the following seven elements are distinct:
  C={0,-u,u,-v,-w,u-v,u-w}.                                  (1)
Let Gamma have left pairs L0_x,L1_x and right pairs R0_y,R1_y,
x,y in K, with Boolean biadjacency matrix

           [ C_(P union Q)     C_Q         ]
           [ C_P              C_(P union Q)].

Then

  mis(Gamma) <= (43177189/20736)^(q/7) < 3^q.                 (2)

In particular, (1) holds for K=F_5^3 with u,v,w independent, proving
the rank-three type B: two disjoint translations of each color. The
same proof applies in every larger ambient space, componentwise or
directly whenever (1) holds. The theorem does not assert (1) for every
rank-two or rank-one configuration.

1. RANDOM MAXIMAL INDEPENDENT SETS AND PAIR STATES

Choose a maximal independent set I uniformly from Gamma. Encode the
selected bits in each left pair by
  Z_x=1_{L0_x in I}+2*1_{L1_x in I} in {0,1,2,3}.

The entire maximal independent set is determined by its left part:
the selected right vertices are exactly the right vertices having no
selected left neighbor. Consequently, using natural-log entropy,

               H((Z_x)_{x in K})=log mis(Gamma).              (3)

Every translation x->x+t of all four rows is a graph automorphism.
Thus every Z_x has the same distribution, say pi, and all translates
of the seven-tuple indexed by C have the same joint entropy.

There is also an involutory graph automorphism

  Phi(L0_x)=R1_{-x},    Phi(L1_x)=R0_{-x},
  Phi(R0_x)=L1_{-x},    Phi(R1_x)=L0_{-x}.                    (4)

For example, an L0_x--R1_y edge has y-x in Q; its image is
L0_{-y}--R1_{-x}, whose difference is also y-x. The P block is checked
the same way and the two union blocks are interchanged. Therefore
the swapped right-pair state

        Z'_y=1_{R1_y in I}+2*1_{R0_y in I}                    (5)

also has distribution pi. This uses uniformity of I under graph
automorphisms, not independence of any selected bits.

2. THE LOCAL NOR MAP

A P-neighbor with state z=x+2y covers right coordinate zero if x or y
is one, and covers right coordinate one if y is one. A Q-neighbor
covers right coordinate zero if x is one, and covers coordinate one
if x or y is one. Maximality makes a right bit one exactly when that
coordinate is not covered.

For two P-input states p0,p1 and two Q-input states q0,q1 define

 F(p0,p1;q0,q1)
   = 1_{p0,p1 in {0,1} and q0=q1=0}
     +2*1_{p0=p1=0 and q0,q1 in {0,2}}.                      (6)

This is exactly the swapped right state in (5). In particular both
indicators in (6) hold precisely when all four inputs are zero.

Write the seven variables in (1) as
  z0=Z_0,       z1=Z_{-u},    z2=Z_u,
  z3=Z_{-v},    z4=Z_{-w},
  z5=Z_{u-v},   z6=Z_{u-w}.

The two right pairs at 0 and u have swapped states
  f0=F(z0,z1;z3,z4),
  f1=F(z0,z2;z5,z6).                                        (7)

They share exactly the central P-input z0. All seven inputs, as well
as f0 and f1, have marginal distribution pi. No assumption about their
joint distribution will be required.

3. AN ELEMENTARY WEIGHTED ENTROPY BOUND

For a random variable X on a finite set and a positive function W,
Jensen's inequality gives
  H(X)+E log W(X) <= log sum_x W(x).                          (8)
Indeed the left side is E log(W(X)/Pr[X]), at most the log of the
sum of W on the support, which is at most the full sum. This proves
the entropy inequality directly, including when some probabilities
are zero.

Use these positive rational weights, ordered by states 0,1,2,3:

 central input: c=(1,1/4,1,1);
 P-leaf input:  a=(1,1/3,2/3,1/2);
 Q-leaf input:  b=(1,2/3,1/3,1/2);
 right output: g=(1,2/27,2/27,1/8).

The exact statewise balance identity is
                       c(s)*a(s)^2*b(s)^4=g(s)^2             (9)
for s=0,1,2,3. For states one and two both sides equal 4/729;
for state three both sides equal 1/64; state zero is immediate.

On all 4^7 possible seven-tuples define

 W(z)= c(z0)a(z1)a(z2)b(z3)b(z4)b(z5)b(z6)
                  /[g(f0)g(f1)],                            (10)

where f0,f1 are computed by (7), whether or not the tuple occurs in
an actual maximal independent set. All weights are strictly positive.
Because all nine random states in (10) have the same marginal pi,
equation (9) gives
                         E log W(Z_C)=0.                    (11)
Apply (8) to obtain
                       H(Z_C)<=log mathcal_Z,
where mathcal_Z=sum_{z in {0,1,2,3}^7} W(z).

4. THE ENTIRE PARTITION SUM IN FOUR ROWS

Fix the shared state z0=s. The two groups of three leaves in (7)
separate completely in (10). Hence

 mathcal_Z=sum_{s=0}^3 c(s) R_s^2,
 R_s=sum_{p,q0,q1=0}^3 a(p)b(q0)b(q1)/g(F(s,p;q0,q1)).        (12)

Each unconstrained triple of leaves has total weight
  (sum a)*(sum b)^2=(5/2)^3=125/8.

For s=0, the output-three part has weight 1, the output-one part has
weight 1/3, and the output-two part has weight
  (1+1/3)^2-1=7/9.
The remaining mass gives output zero. Thus
  R_0=125/8-1-1/3-7/9+8+(27/2)(1/3+7/9)=2629/72.

For s=1, output one occurs exactly when p in {0,1} and q0=q1=0,
of weight 4/3. All other inputs give output zero. Thus
  R_1=125/8+(27/2-1)(4/3)=775/24.

For s=2 or 3, the P-input s forces both indicators in (6) to vanish,
so R_2=R_3=125/8. The certificate table is therefore

          s       c(s)        R_s
          0        1        2629/72
          1       1/4        775/24
          2        1         125/8
          3        1         125/8.

Substitution in (12) gives the exact value

 mathcal_Z=(2629/72)^2+(1/4)(775/24)^2+2(125/8)^2
          =43,177,189/20,736
          <45,349,632/20,736
          =2187=3^7.                                        (13)

The supplementary verifier enumerates all 16,384 configurations in
(10) using a separate direct adjacency/NOR routine and obtains the
same rational number. The four-row computation itself proves (13).

5. THE TRANSLATED ENTROPY COVER

Consider the q translates C+t, t in K. Because (1) consists of seven
distinct coordinates, every coordinate x in K occurs in exactly
seven translates, counting translates with multiplicity.

For completeness, the required entropy covering inequality follows
directly from the chain rule. Order the coordinates of K. For any
subset D,
  H(Z_D)=sum_{x in D} H(Z_x | earlier coordinates in D)
       >=sum_{x in D} H(Z_x | all earlier coordinates of K),
since conditioning cannot increase entropy. Summing over all C+t
counts the terms on the right seven times and gives

    7 H((Z_x)_{x in K}) <= sum_{t in K} H(Z_{C+t}).             (14)

Translation invariance makes every entropy on the right equal to
H(Z_C). Combining (3), (11)--(14),

 log mis(Gamma) <= (q/7) H(Z_C)
                <= (q/7) log(43177189/20736)
                 < q log 3.

Exponentiating proves (2). No classification of maximal independent
sets, numerical optimization, or asymptotic passage is needed in this
local graph theorem.

7. ALL TWO-BY-TWO DISJOINT COLOR SETS OVER F_5

COROLLARY. Let K be any finite-dimensional F_5-vector space. If P,Q are
disjoint two-element subsets of K, then mis Gamma(P,Q)<=3^{|K|}.

Simultaneous translation of P,Q only relabels the right columns.
Exchanging P,Q only swaps both left clone blocks and both right clone
blocks. We may therefore write P={0,u}, Q={v,w}, with u!=0 and
v,w distinct and outside {0,u}.

COLLISION CLASSIFICATION. The seven coordinates in (1) fail to be
distinct if and only if at least one of these events occurs:

  v or w belongs to {-u,2u};                                (15)
  w-v belongs to {u,-u}.                                    (16)

Proof. The three coordinates 0,-u,u are distinct, and each pair
{-v,u-v}, {-w,u-w} has two distinct entries. Neither pair contains
zero, since v,w are outside {0,u}. Its possible intersections with
{-u,u} are exactly v in {-u,2u} or w in {-u,2u}; the other equations
would force v or w into {0,u}. Finally an intersection between the two
two-element pairs gives v=w or w-v=+/-u. The first is excluded. This
lists every possible pairwise collision and proves both directions.

If (16) holds, Q is a translate of P. This parallel case is handled
below by a Boolean factorization.

Suppose (16) does not hold. If (15) also does not hold, the entropy
theorem applies immediately. If (15) holds, rename the two Q elements
so that v in {-u,2u}, and put L=span{u}. When w also lies in L, all
four translations have rank one, a case treated below. Otherwise
w is outside L. Exchange the colors and subtract v from both sets.
The new normalized sets are

      P_new={0,z}, z=w-v outside L,
      Q_new={-v,u-v} subset L\{0}.                           (17)

Since L intersects span{z} only in zero, neither new Q element can
be -z or 2z. The difference of the two Q elements is u, which cannot
be +z or -z. Thus neither (15) nor (16) holds for (17). Its seven
coordinates are distinct, so the same entropy theorem applies.
This covers every affine-rank-at-least-two nonparallel configuration.

PARALLEL CASE: A GENERAL BOOLEAN FACTORIZATION LEMMA.
For sets R,A,B in a finite abelian group, write R+A for their Minkowski
sum. Boolean multiplication of Cayley matrices gives
  C_R C_A=C_(R+A).
Therefore

 GammaMatrix(R+A,R+B)
   =diag(C_R,C_R) GammaMatrix(A,B).                          (18)

Here all products are Boolean OR/AND products. The row-union image of
a Boolean product XY is contained in the row-union image of Y, since
any row union has the form zXY=(zX)Y. The number of maximal independent
sets of a bipartite graph equals the number of distinct row unions.
Thus (18) implies

 mis Gamma(R+A,R+B)<=mis Gamma(A,B).                         (19)

For completeness, the row-union identity follows by taking a row-union
U and the right set Y=R\U, then the maximal left set L\N(Y). This is
an independent dominating set with right part Y. Conversely every
maximal independent set has exactly these two parts for its row union.

If Q=P+t, take R=P,A={0},B={t} in (19). This reduces the parallel
case to Gamma({0},{t}). If t=0 the matrix has paired twin rows and the
bound is immediate. If t!=0, it has order five, and the matrix splits
into |K|/5 components indexed by cosets of span{t}, each with 20
vertices. Each such component obeys the following small paper bound.

RANK-ONE / TWENTY-VERTEX BOUND.
Any Gamma(P,Q) on F_5 with nonempty P,Q has a perfect C4 factor:
choose p in P,q in Q and, for each x, pair L0_x,L1_x with
R0_(x+p),R1_(x+q). All four cross edges are present and the five
blocks partition the vertices.

For a maximal independent set, let a be the number of occupied left
pairs and b the number of occupied right pairs. The C4 factor implies
a+b<=5. If a<=2, its left part has at most
  sum_{i=0}^2 binom(5,i)3^i=1+15+90=106
possibilities. If a>=3, then b<=2 and its right part has at most 106
possibilities. A maximal independent set is determined by either side,
so altogether there are at most 212<243=3^5 possibilities.

This proves the needed parallel bound by multiplication over the
20-vertex components. The same argument handles the earlier rank-one
case, because all four translations lie in L and again decompose into
20-vertex components. All cases of the corollary are now proved.

The classification uses only the explicit collision equations and the
Boolean factorization, not a list of rank-two affine representatives.
The optional verifier checks the collision criterion throughout
F_5^3 and checks the Boolean factorization on a full rank-two model.

As elsewhere, a generic-component estimate does not by itself remove
the bounded-size exceptional core from the original sum-free-set
problem. The corollary is the required full type-B matrix theorem.
