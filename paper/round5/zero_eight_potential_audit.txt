ROUND 5: INDEPENDENT AUDIT OF THE DEGREE-EIGHT TRIANGLE-FREE POTENTIAL
Status: all stated claims below proved and independently checked.
The certificate zero_verify_eight.py uses integers and rational numbers only.
No Lean formalization or claim about research novelty is made.

1. THE HEREDITARY CLASS AND THE THEOREM

Let C_8 be the class of finite simple triangle-free graphs F such that
  (i) maximum degree is at most eight;
  (ii) every degree-eight vertex has at least three neighbors of degree
       at most seven.
Set
        mu(F) = (16 |V(F)| - |E(F)|)/31.
Then
        mis(F) <= 2^{mu(F)}.                                  (1)

The class is hereditary under induced subgraphs. Indeed, if a vertex
has degree eight in an induced subgraph, it had degree exactly eight
in F, and all its original neighbors remain. At least three of those
neighbors had degree at most seven in F, and vertex deletion cannot
increase their degrees. Triangle-freeness is also hereditary.

The potential is additive over components, and maximal-independent-set
counts multiply over components. It therefore suffices to deal with
connected graphs. The proof below is induction on the number of vertices;
every application of an inductive estimate is to a proper induced graph.
The intermediate strengthened estimates are obtained directly by applying
that induction to their branches, so there is no circularity.

For a vertex v of degree d,
        mu(F)-mu(F-v) = (16-d)/31.                            (2)
In a triangle-free graph, the number of edges removed with N[v] is
exactly sum_{u in N(v)} deg(u). Hence, if d is the current maximum degree,
        mu(F)-mu(F-N[v]) >= [16(d+1)-d^2]/31.                 (3)
Use the usual inequality
        mis(F) <= mis(F-v)+mis(F-N[v]).

2. CURRENT MAXIMUM DEGREE FIVE THROUGH EIGHT

For d=5,6,7, the drops in (2),(3) and rational upper bounds for the
corresponding powers of two are:

    d       numerator drops /31      first bound    second bound
    5             11,71                 79/100         21/100
    6             10,76                 81/100         19/100
    7              9,79                 41/50           9/50

Every displayed exponential bound is strict, and each pair sums to one.

For d=8, at least three neighbors have degree at most seven. Thus the
number of edges removed with N[v] is at most
        5*8+3*7=61.
The two potential drops are at least 8/31 and 83/31. The exponential
factors are strictly smaller than 21/25 and 4/25, respectively. This
also closes the induction.

3. CURRENT MAXIMUM DEGREE FOUR

First consider a connected graph with maximum degree four that is not
four-regular. Along a path from a degree-four vertex to a smaller-degree
vertex, there is a degree-four vertex v with a neighbor of degree at
most three. Thus at most 15 edges meet N[v], and the two drops are
12/31 and at least 65/31. The factors are strictly below 49/64 and 15/64,
whose sum is one.

The four-regular case uses an ordered closed-neighborhood branching
estimate, recorded next in a form that will also treat cubic graphs.

ORDERED-NEIGHBORHOOD ESTIMATE.
Let F be triangle-free, with maximum degree at most d. Suppose x has
degree d-1 and its neighbors y_1,...,y_{d-1} all have degree d.
Partition the maximal independent sets according to the first selected
vertex in the list x,y_1,...,y_{d-1}. For index r=0, delete N[x]. For
r>=1, delete
        N[y_r] union {y_1,...,y_{r-1}}.
The count in each part injects into the maximal independent sets of the
corresponding induced remaining graph: the selected vertex dominates its
closed neighborhood, and the extra deleted vertices were excluded.
Every remaining unselected vertex still has a selected neighbor in the
remaining graph.

For r=0, the deleted set has d vertices and meets d(d-1) edges. For
r>=1, triangle-freeness makes the y_i pairwise nonadjacent, so the
deleted set has exactly d+r vertices. The closed neighborhood N[y_r]
meets at most d^2-1 edges, because one neighbor is x of degree d-1.
Each of the r-1 extra vertices adds at most d-1 incident edges: its
edge to x has already been counted. Therefore the total is at most
        d^2-1+(r-1)(d-1)=(d+r)(d-1).
In every case the potential drop is at least
        (d+r)(17-d)/31,       r=0,...,d-1.                    (4)

Apply this with d=4. The four drops have numerators 52,65,78,91.
The exponential factors are strictly below
        8/25, 6/25, 9/50, 27/200,
whose sum is 7/8. Thus any such local degree-three configuration gives
        mis(F) < (7/8) 2^{mu(F)}.                            (5)

Now let F be connected, triangle-free and four-regular. Delete any v.
Every neighbor x of v has degree three in F-v, and each of x's remaining
neighbors has degree four: otherwise such a neighbor was adjacent to v,
forming a triangle in F. Therefore (5) applies to F-v. Deleting v drops
the potential by 12/31; deleting N[v] drops it by 64/31>2. The total
coefficient is strictly below
        (7/8)(49/64)+1/4 = 471/512 < 1.
This completes the maximum-degree-four cases.

4. SUBCUBIC GRAPHS

Isolated vertices contribute count one and positive potential. An
isolated edge has potential one and count two, so is exact.

NON-ISOLATED LEAF.
Let x be a leaf with neighbor y of degree d=2 or 3. Exactly
        mis(F)=mis(F-{x,y})+mis(F-N[y]).
For the first branch, at most d edges are removed, giving drops 30/31
for d=2 and 29/31 for d=3. In the second branch, the sum of the degrees
of y's neighbors is at most 1+3(d-1), so the drops are at least 44/31
and 57/31, respectively. Consequently the two factors are uniformly
strictly below 21/40 and 3/8, whose sum is 9/10. Thus
        mis(F) < (9/10) 2^{mu(F)}                            (6)
whenever a subcubic graph contains a leaf whose neighbor is not a leaf.

MIXED DEGREES TWO AND THREE.
If a connected subcubic graph has minimum degree at least two and is
neither a cycle nor cubic, choose a degree-three vertex y adjacent to
a degree-two vertex x. Let z be x's other neighbor. Triangle-freeness
ensures yz is absent, so in F-y the vertex x is a leaf whose neighbor z
still has degree at least two. Apply (6) to this branch. The delete-y
drop is 13/31, and the closed-neighborhood drop is at least 56/31,
since the neighbor-degree sum is at most 2+3+3=8. The ordinary factors
are strictly below 3/4 and 3/10. The resulting coefficient is less than
        (9/10)(3/4)+3/10=39/40<1.                           (7)

CUBIC GRAPHS.
Use the ordered estimate (4) with d=3. Its drops have numerators
42,56,70, and their factors are strictly below
        2/5, 29/100, 21/100,
whose sum is 9/10. In a triangle-free cubic graph, deleting any v leaves
one of its former neighbors with degree two and both remaining
neighbors of degree three. Thus this 9/10 estimate applies to F-v.
The root drops are 13/31 and 55/31, with factors below 3/4 and 3/10.
Again the total is less than 39/40.

CYCLES.
For C_l, l>=4, write a_l=mis(C_l). Then
        a_4=2, a_5=5, a_6=5,
        a_l=a_{l-2}+a_{l-3} for l>=7.
For example, encode each selected vertex by state 0 and the one or two
unselected vertices following it by states 1 and 2. The allowed cyclic
transition matrix is
        T=[[0,1,0],[1,0,1],[1,0,0]].
The count is tr(T^l), and T^3=T+I proves the recurrence. Set rho=25/18.
The three initial counts are strictly smaller than rho^l, and
        rho^(-2)+rho^(-3)=13932/15625<1.
Thus a_l<rho^l by induction. The integer comparison
        25^31 < 18^31 * 2^15
implies rho<2^{15/31}. As E(C_l)=l, this gives a_l<2^{mu(C_l)}.

These cases exhaust all graphs of maximum degree at most three.
Together with Sections 2 and 3, they prove (1).

5. APPLICATION: TWO INDEPENDENT ZERO SEEDS AND ONE NONZERO SEED

Let G=F_5 x H, B={2,3} x H, and
        S={(0,e_1),(0,e_2),(1,0)},
where e_1,e_2 are linearly independent in H. A nonzero seed in layer one
can always be put into this form by a shear preserving the zero layer
and B; a seed in layer four is reduced to layer one by negation first.
Set K=span{e_1,e_2}. Choose t not in K and consider the component over
the two distinct cosets t+K and -t+K. It consists of the following four
rows, indexed by x in K:

        a_x=(2,t+x),       b_x=(3,t+x),
        c_x=(3,-t-x),      d_x=(2,-t-x).

The complete edge list is:
  * within each row, x is joined to x+/-e_1 and x+/-e_2;
  * a_x c_{x-e_i} and b_x d_{x-e_i}, i=1,2;
  * a_x b_x, b_x c_x, c_x d_x.

These formulas follow directly from the link conditions u+v in S or
u-v in S or v-u in S. There are no loops in this component: all loop
vertices arising from S lie over K (in fact the only loop vertices here
are (2,0) and (3,0), both in the exceptional core).

The a,d rows have degree seven and the b,c rows have degree eight.
Every b_x has exactly the following three degree-seven neighbors:
        a_x, d_{x-e_1}, d_{x-e_2}.
Every c_x has exactly the following three degree-seven neighbors:
        d_x, a_{x+e_1}, a_{x+e_2}.

The graph is triangle-free, as follows.
  * A single row is C_5 square C_5 and contains no triangle.
  * A two-row triangle cannot use an identity matching. For the a,c or
    b,d row pairs, two cross neighbors differ by e_1-e_2, which is not
    one of +/-e_1,+/-e_2 by linear independence.
  * The only possible triples of distinct rows are a,b,c and b,c,d.
    The two identity-matching edges force all three indices to coincide,
    whereas the remaining cross edge requires a nonzero shift e_i.
    This is impossible.

Thus the component lies in C_8. It has 100 vertices, 50 in each degree
class, and 375 edges. Theorem (1) gives the requested paper bound
        mis(component) <= 2^{(1600-375)/31}
                       = 2^{1225/31}
                       < 3^{25}.                            (8)
The strict comparison in (8) is exactly 2^49<3^31.

This argument proves the generic-component assertion requested in the
audit. The exceptional core over K is not claimed to obey (8), and no
new bound on that core is inferred from generic-component estimates.
If the two zero seeds are dependent and sum-free, they are opposite;
that separate case is already covered by the symmetric-core plus one
defect theorem from round 4.

6. EXACT CERTIFICATE CONVENTION

Every exponential bound 2^{-a/31}<p/q in this proof is equivalent to
        q^31 < p^31 * 2^a.
All these comparisons, all rational coefficient sums, the three cycle
initial values, the cycle growth comparison, and the final target
comparison are verified with integers/rational numbers in
zero_verify_eight.py. The script additionally reconstructs the 100-point
graph both from the row formulas and independently from the original
F_5^4 link conditions, compares the two adjacency lists, and verifies
its exact degree counts, its three low-degree neighbors per high-degree
vertex, and the absence of triangles. It does not enumerate maximal
independent sets, and the numerical checks do not replace the proof.
