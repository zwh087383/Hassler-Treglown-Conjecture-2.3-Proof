For P={0,u},Q={v,w}, the right centers 0 and u see respectively
  (Z_0,Z_{-u};Z_{-v},Z_{-w}),

Thus the common input is exactly Z_0 when the seven listed coordinates
are distinct. In characteristic five, independent u,v,w ensure that
distinctness. The graph automorphism L0_x<->R1_{-x}, L1_x<->R0_{-x}
preserves each P,Q block and interchanges the union blocks. Together
with translations it proves that every input and each swapped output
has one common marginal law pi. No joint-distribution assumption is
being silently used.

For P-input state z, the covered-right mask is (0,1,3,3)[z]; for Q-input
it is (0,3,2,3)[z]. OR of the masks followed by the map (3,1,2,0)
gives the swapped right state. This reproduces formula (6) in the
original proof exactly, including output three when all inputs vanish.

5. The subsequently supplied geometric completion: all affine ranks.
Normalize P={0,u}, Q={v,w}, with the four elements distinct and u!=0.
In characteristic five, a direct pairwise comparison shows that the
seven coordinates fail to be distinct if and only if
  v or w belongs to {-u,2u}, or w-v belongs to {u,-u}.
All other possible equalities would force u=0, v=w, or one of v,w to
equal 0 or u, which the normalization excludes.

If w-v=+/-u, then Q=P+t for some t. With C_P the Boolean translation
matrix, exact Boolean matrix multiplication gives
  M(P,Q)=diag(C_P,C_P) M({0},{t}).
For any bipartite matrix, the number of maximal independent sets equals
the number of distinct unions of its rows. Every row union of a Boolean
product AB is a row union of B. Thus the displayed factorization gives
  mis M(P,Q)<=mis M({0},{t})<=3^q,
using the proved two-permutation/even-prism theorem. This is a row-union
inclusion, not a claim of general monotonicity under adding edges.

Otherwise suppose v is one of -u,2u; rename v,w if needed. Let
L=span{u}. If the affine rank is at least two, w lies outside L, since
0,u,v already lie in L. Swap P and Q, then translate by -v. The new
parameters are
  u'=w-v outside L, v'=-v in L\{0}, w'=u-v in L\{0}.
Neither v' nor w' can equal -u' or 2u', and w'-v'=u cannot equal
u' or -u'. Hence the new seven-coordinate shape has no collision.
The entropy theorem applies to the relabeled isomorphic graph.

Finally, if the affine rank is one, work on the five-element span.
The bipartite graph has ten vertices on each side and every row
neighborhood has exactly six elements. There is one empty row union.
A union of size six must equal one of the at most ten row neighborhoods.
All unions of size at least seven contribute at most
  binom(10,7)+binom(10,8)+binom(10,9)+binom(10,10)=176.
Consequently mis<=1+10+176=187<243=3^5. Extra ambient coordinates
give disjoint copies of this graph, so the inequality multiplies.

These three cases prove the entire disjoint two-by-two type B on paper.
The earlier rank-two DAG certificates are not needed as a logical
dependency of this completed proof.

6. Audit of the final self-contained Section 7 as saved by matrix.
The final manuscript uses a unified 212 bound instead of the separate
187 bound above and an earlier prism reference. This replacement is
also correct and is fully self-contained. For a Gamma(P,Q) matrix on
F_5, choose p in P and q in Q. The five blocks with left pair
{L0_x,L1_x} and right pair {R0_{x+p},R1_{x+q}} form a C4 factor.
For any independent set the number of occupied left blocks plus the
number of occupied right blocks is at most five. Hence one side
occupies at most two blocks, with at most
  1+5*3+binom(5,2)*3^2=106
choices for that side. Since an MIS is determined by either whole
side, this yields at most 212<243 MISs. The right pairing need not be
the natural same-coordinate pairing; this does not affect the count.
This one paragraph applies both to the rank-one B matrix and to each
order-five component of the two-seed Boolean factor. All of the final
Section 7 is therefore independently approved without any old counting
