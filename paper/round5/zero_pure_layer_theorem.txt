THE COMPLETE PURE-ZERO-LAYER LINK-GRAPH THEOREM
==============================================
This theorem is now a paper consequence of the fifth-round potential
bounds and the earlier single-defect/two-seed paper results. The previous
rank-two exhaustive computation is not needed for the proof below.

Theorem. Let H be any finite vector space over F_5 and let A subset H be
sum-free, with repeated summands allowed. Put G=F_5 x H, h=|H|, n=5h,
B={2,3}xH, and S={0}xA. Then
    mis(L_S[B]) <= 3^(h/2)=3^(n/10).
There is no restriction on |A| or dim span(A), no exceptional core
factor, and no asymptotic error in this local bound.

Inputs used.
1. The earlier pure-zero paper proofs for |A|<=2, with no exceptional
   core: singleton/opposite-pair components are explicit, and the two
   independent seeds use the five-cycle folding lemma on each 50-point
   component. These give the exact local target bound.
2. The fourth-round paper theorem for symmetric seeds and for seeds with
   at most one element unpaired under negation.
3. The root fifth-round general regular-graph theorem: if a simple graph
   is d-regular with d>=10, then
       mis(F) <= 2^((c-27/200)|F|) < 3^(|F|/4),
   where c=log_2(3)/3. The comparison follows from 3^50<2^81.
4. global_trianglefree_potential.txt, Theorem 1 (appendix P2): every
   triangle-free graph of maximum degree at most ten satisfies
       mis(F) <= 2^[|V(F)|/2-(|E(F)|-|V(F)|/2)/36].
   For a nine-regular graph on N vertices this is
       mis(F) <= 2^(7N/18) < 3^(N/4),
   where the strict comparison is the integer inequality 2^14<3^9.
5. zero_triple_proof.txt: the nine-regular graph generated by exactly
   three distinct zero-sum vectors in a pure-zero layer has
       mis(F) <= 2^((c-133/1000)|F|) < 3^(|F|/4).
Inputs 4 and 5 have independent exact scalar certificates. Input 3 is
proved in the root's general potential manuscript and was independently
audited by the global agent. No new Lean certification is asserted here.

Proof.
The link graph has N=2h vertices and no loops, because S+S, S-S, and
(1/2)S are contained in the zero layer, disjoint from B. Write its
vertices as X_x=(2,x), Y_x=(3,-x). If D=A union (-A), its edges are
    X_x X_{x+d}, Y_x Y_{x+d}  (d in D),
    X_x Y_{x-a}              (a in A).
Thus the graph is regular of degree
    d=|D|+|A|=3|A|-|A intersect (-A)|.
Put m=|A| and write |A intersect (-A)|=2p, where p is the number of
opposite pairs. Since 2p<=m, we have d=3m-2p>=2m.

If d>=10, Input 3 gives the result directly. Otherwise m<=4.
For m<=2 use Input 1. For m=4, the inequality d<=9 forces p=2, so A=-A
and Input 2 applies. For m=3, either p=1 and A has exactly one unpaired
element, again covered by Input 2, or p=0 and d=9.

It remains to handle m=3,p=0,d=9. We first record the exact criterion:
    L_{ {0}xA }[B] is triangle-free iff 0 is not in A+A+A.     (1)
There are no mixed-row triangles. Such a triangle would give a,b in A
and a-b in +/-A, hence an equation between three elements of A of the
form u+v=w. A same-row triangle gives a zero-sum triple of signed
members of A. Mixed signs again yield a Schur relation within A; the
only possibility is that all signs agree, giving 0 in A+A+A.
Conversely, a+b+c=0 with a,b,c in A gives a same-row triangle on offsets
0,a,a+b; these vertices are distinct because 0 is not in A.
This proves (1).

If 0 is not in A+A+A, Input 4 completes the proof. If 0 is in A+A+A,
a zero-sum triple cannot repeat an entry: 2a+b=0 would imply 2b=a in
characteristic five, contradicting sum-freeness. Hence the three
entries are exactly the three distinct members of A. They span rank
two: their sum gives a dependence, while a one-dimensional subspace has
only four nonzero vectors split into two opposite pairs, so three
nonzero members there would contain an opposite pair, incompatible
with a zero sum whose third entry is nonzero. Therefore, after a linear
change of coordinates,
    A={a,b,-a-b},   a,b independent.
Input 5 applies. All cases are covered. QED.

The theorem resolves the entire pure-zero-layer portion of the desired
local MIS bound. It does not prove the full maximal sum-free-set counting
conjecture, since mixed zero/nonzero-layer seeds remain to be controlled.
