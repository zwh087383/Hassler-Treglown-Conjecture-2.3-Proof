1. Coordinate edge table.
For X_x=(2,x), Y_x=(3,-x), A=S_0, P=S_1, Q=-S_4, U=P union Q:
  XX differences give A union -A; XX sums give -Q.
  YY differences give A union -A; YY sums give -P.
  XY sums give x-y in A; the two XY differences give x+y in -U.
Thus all signs in the table are correct. Translation edges preserve the
two coset signs and reflection edges exchange them. As g not in K and
2g not in K, the two types cannot overlap, and reflection edges have no
fixed point. This also verifies the degree formulas and the equal sizes
of the two row classes. Swapping the rows replaces (A,P,Q) by (-A,Q,P),
so assuming p>=q is legitimate.

2. Triangle-free lemma and its sum-free consequences.
A closed triangle has zero or two reflections. Zero reflections give a
sum of three increments from {t,-t}, which cannot vanish in F_5. Two
reflections with labels u,v require u-v=+/-t along the translation edge.
The stated shift-free condition on U-U therefore excludes all triangles.

When P,Q are nested, U is one of the two sets, and a difference +/-t
would create a Schur triple with whichever of t,-t lies in A. If A is
the full opposite pair, differences within P or Q are excluded as above,
and a difference across P and Q is excluded by (P-Q) intersect A empty.
Both consequences are valid, including the empty nonzero-layer case.

3. The uniform neighborhood-triangle dichotomy.
For A={t} and v=u+t with u,v in U, the proposed edges in N(Y_y) are
  Y_{y+t} -- X_{-y-v}, with coordinate sum -u;
  Y_{y-t} -- X_{-y-u}, with coordinate sum -v.
Both are cross-row reflection edges. The four endpoints are genuine
neighbors of Y_y. The two edges are distinct since t!=-t and u!=v.
The argument works in either generic coset sign and for every Y_y.

4. Degree-cap lemmas.
In 2A a current degree-d vertex with d>9 must be originally high. It
has at most six high neighbors and hence at least d-6 low neighbors.
The bound d^2-(d-9)(d-6)=15d-54 on edges meeting N[v] is valid even
when the graph has triangles. With beta=c/17 the two potential drops
are exactly bounded below by c(18-d)/17 and c(3d+72)/17. The fixed-beta
master lemma applies to all residual graphs of maximum degree at most
nine. The initial potential is (3c/4)n as claimed.

In 2B any vertex retaining degree ten retains its entire original
neighborhood. In particular it retains the five low neighbors and the
two edges among neighbors. Consequently at most 5*10+5*9-2=93 edges
meet N[v]. For beta=c/15 the drops are 2c/5 and 83c/15. The same fixed
beta is used in the residual maximum-degree-nine master theorem.
Again the initial potential is (3c/4)n. All nine comparison certificates
and all three degree-cap polynomial checks passed exactly.
