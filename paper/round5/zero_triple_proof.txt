A PAPER CERTIFICATE FOR THE ZERO-SUM THREE-SEED GRAPH
==================================================
Status: completed paper proof. No enumeration of the 50-vertex graph's
maximal independent sets is used. No new Lean verification is claimed.

1. A hereditary graph class and its potential
---------------------------------------------
Set
    c=(log_2 3)/3,   beta=19/500,
    mu(F)=(c+beta)v(F)-beta e(F).
Let C be the class of finite simple graphs with maximum degree at most
nine such that every vertex of degree nine has at least six edges within
its open neighborhood.

Theorem. Every graph F in C satisfies
    mis(F) <= 2^mu(F).                                        (1)

The class C is hereditary under induced vertex deletion. Indeed, a
vertex retaining degree nine in an induced subgraph of F must originally
have had degree nine, so none of its original neighbors was removed.
All original edges among those neighbors are consequently retained.

For later use define A_d and B_d by
    A_d=c-beta(d-1),
    B_d=(d+1)c-beta(d^2-d-1).
Deleting a degree-d vertex decreases mu by exactly A_d. If d is the
maximum degree, deleting its closed neighborhood decreases mu by at
least B_d. The latter statement needs no triangle-free assumption: the
number of removed edges equals
    sum_{u in N(v)}deg(u)-e(F[N(v)]) <= d^2.

We use the standard relations
    mis(F) <= mis(F-v)+mis(F-N[v]),                            (2)
and, if v is a leaf with neighbor w,
    mis(F)=mis(F-{v,w})+mis(F-N[w]).                           (3)
For any independent set Z, the maximal independent sets containing Z
are counted exactly by mis(F-N[Z]). All inequalities involving scalar
powers below have strict integer certificates in zero_verify_triple.py.

2. High-degree steps
--------------------
For maximum degree d=4,...,8, the inductive coefficient in (2) is
    f(d)=2^(-A_d)+2^(-B_d).
This is convex in real d: both summands are exponentials of convex
functions (one affine, one quadratic). Thus it is enough to check d=4
and d=8. We have the strict rational estimates
    2^(-A_4)<10/13,  2^(-B_4)<3/13,
    2^(-A_8)<21/25,  2^(-B_8)<4/25.
Their sums are strictly less than one.

For maximum degree nine choose a degree-nine vertex v. By the defining
property of C, deleting N[v] removes ten vertices and at most
    9^2-6=75
edges. Its potential decrease is therefore at least 10c-65beta. The
exclude decrease is c-8beta. Their coefficients satisfy
    2^(-(c-8beta))<6/7,
    2^(-(10c-65beta))<1/7,
and again their sum is strictly less than one.

3. Low-degree induction, including triangles
--------------------------------------------
We provide the full low-degree argument; in particular, no triangle-free
hypothesis is imposed in this section. All subsidiary estimates are
derived using (1) only on strictly smaller graphs.

The potential is additive over components and the count multiplicative,
so disconnected graphs follow by induction on their proper components.
An isolated vertex is forced and its deletion decreases mu by c+beta>0.
An isolated edge contributes exactly two sets and has potential
2c+beta>1, so it too is harmless.

A. A leaf with neighbor degree two or three.
In (3), if deg(w)=2, the potential decreases are at least 2c and 3c-beta.
If deg(w)=3, they are at least 2c-beta and 4c-3beta. These follow by counting
at most 1+3=4 or 1+3+3=7 removed edges in the closed-neighborhood branch.
Since 4c-3beta >= 3c-beta, uniformly
    mis(F) < (17/20) 2^mu(F).                                (4)
Indeed
    2^(-(2c-beta))<1/2,
    2^(-(3c-beta))<7/20,
and 1/2+7/20=17/20.

B. A locally triangle-free degree-two/degree-three configuration.
Suppose x has degree two, with neighbors y,z, where deg(y)=3,
deg(z)>=2, and yz is not an edge. Deleting y makes x a leaf while z
keeps its original degree, so (4) applies to F-y. The two potential
decreases in (2) at y are at least c-2beta and 4c-4beta; for the latter,
the sum of neighbor degrees is at most 2+3+3=8. Hence
    mis(F)/2^mu(F)
      < (17/20)2^(-(c-2beta))+2^(-(4c-4beta))
      < (17/20)(3/4)+13/50
       =359/400 <19/20.                                     (5)

C. A triangle containing a degree-two vertex.
Suppose the graph has minimum degree at least two, maximum degree at
most three, and a degree-two vertex x next to a degree-three vertex y.
If the other neighbor z of x is adjacent to y, these vertices form a
triangle. If z also has degree two, this is a triangle with exactly one
external edge, incident with y. Every maximal independent set selects
exactly one vertex of this triangle, since x,z have no outside neighbor.
The branches selecting x or z delete the triangle and its one outgoing
edge, giving potential decrease 3c-beta. The branch selecting y deletes
four vertices and at most six edges, giving decrease at least 4c-2beta.
Thus the coefficient is less than
    2(7/20)+1/4=19/20<1.

If z has degree three, the triangle has two outgoing edges. Again every
maximal independent set selects a triangle vertex, because x has no
outside neighbor. Selecting x decreases mu by 3c-2beta; selecting y or
z decreases it by at least 4c-3beta. Since
    2^(-(3c-2beta))<9/25,
and 4c-3beta>4c-4beta, the sum is less than
    9/25+2(13/50)=22/25<1.
If all vertices of a component have degree two, it is a cycle. For a
cycle e(F)=v(F), so mu(F)=c v(F), and the classical Moon--Moser bound
mis(F)<=3^(v(F)/3) is exactly sufficient. This also handles the component
K3 with equality.

D. Cubic graphs with no triangle.
If F is cubic and triangle-free, delete any vertex v. A neighbor x of v
now has degree two, while each of its other neighbors still has degree
three: otherwise there was a triangle through v. The configuration in
B is present in F-v. Thus the first term in (2) benefits from (5).
The two potential decreases are c-2beta and 4c-5beta. The coefficient is
less than
    (19/20)(3/4)+4/15=47/48<1,                               (6)
using 2^(-(4c-5beta))<4/15.

E. Cubic graphs containing a triangle.
Let x,y,z form a triangle. Each has exactly one neighbor outside the
triangle. If all three outside neighbors coincide, the graph has a K4
component. Its four maximal independent sets satisfy the bound because
    mu(K4)=4c-2beta>2.
Otherwise there are at least two distinct outside neighbors.

A maximal independent set either selects one of x,y,z or selects none.
There are three branches of the first kind. In each, deleting the closed
neighborhood removes four vertices and at most eight edges: the two
other triangle vertices already contribute an edge inside the open
neighborhood. Thus each branch decreases mu by at least 4c-4beta.
In the branch selecting no triangle vertex, all distinct outside
neighbors are forced to be selected. If they are not independent this
branch is empty. Otherwise delete their closed neighborhoods. This
deletes at least the three triangle vertices and two outside vertices,
so at least five vertices. In a graph of maximum degree three, deleting
u vertices removes at most 3u edges, hence decreases mu by at least
    u(c-2beta) >= 5(c-2beta).
The combined coefficient is consequently less than
    3(13/50)+21/100=99/100<1,                                (7)
using 2^(-5(c-2beta))<21/100.

Completion of induction.
Once isolated vertices and leaves have been handled, a connected
subcubic graph is either a cycle, cubic, or has adjacent vertices of
degrees two and three. The latter configuration is covered by B or C,
and the cubic cases by D or E. Together with Section 2 these exhaust
all graphs in C, proving (1). QED.

4. Application to a zero-sum triple in F_5^k
-------------------------------------------
Let a,b be linearly independent in a vector space H over F_5 and put
    c0=-a-b,   A={a,b,c0}.
Set S={0}xA and B={2,3}xH. This seed is sum-free, has no opposite pair,
and its link graph has no loops. With X_x=(2,x), Y_x=(3,-x), each vertex
has six same-row neighbors in directions +/-a,+/-b,+/-c0 and three
opposite-row neighbors. The graph is therefore nine-regular.

In each vertex's six same-row neighbors there is a cycle of length six.
At a center x, the offsets in order are
    a, -b, c0, -a, b, -c0, a.
Consecutive differences are again in +/-A; the six offsets are distinct
by independence of a,b. Hence every degree-nine vertex has at least six
edges within its neighborhood. The link graph belongs to C.

Writing N=2|H| and using e(F)=9N/2 in (1), we obtain
    mis(L_S[B]) <= 2^((c-133/1000)N) < 3^(N/4).               (8)
The strict final comparison is equivalent to
    c<133/250,
or the exact integer inequality
    3^250 < 2^399.

For H=F_5^2, N=50, the requested small graph thus has the purely paper
bound
    mis(F_A) <= 2^(50(log_2 3)/3 - 133/20) < 3^(25/2).
No exact MIS count such as 19245 is needed in this proof. The same
inequality applies directly in any larger ambient space H.

5. Why this is the only remaining low-degree pure-zero pattern
--------------------------------------------------------------
For a general pure-zero sum-free A, put m=|A| and let p be the number of
opposite pairs in A. Its link graph is regular of degree
    d=3m-2p>=2m.
Thus d<=9 implies m<=4. If m=4, d<=9 forces p=2 and A=-A. If m=3,
either p=1 (one unpaired element, already proved), or p=0 and d=9.
In the latter case, if 0 is not in A+A+A the graph is triangle-free and
the earlier ninth-degree potential applies. If 0 is in A+A+A, no repeated
summand is possible: 2a+b=0 would imply 2b=a in characteristic five,
contradicting sum-freeness. Hence A consists of three distinct elements
summing to zero. They cannot lie in a line: its four nonzero vectors split into two
opposite pairs, so any three distinct vectors contain an opposite pair,
incompatible with their zero sum and nonzero third member. Thus A is
exactly the rank-two pattern in Section 4, up to an invertible linear map.
The cases m<=2 have the earlier paper proofs.

Once the independently established arbitrary-regular-graph degree-at-
least-ten theorem is attached, this classification gives the full pure-
zero seed theorem in every rank without invoking the previous finite
rank-two enumeration. That final attachment is recorded by root.
